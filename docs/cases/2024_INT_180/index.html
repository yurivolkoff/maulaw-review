12JUDGMENT
131. The Plaintiff is suing the Defendant for damages on the ground of “faute lourde”.
14In his amended proecipe, the Plaintiff avers that he was arrested in April 2014
15during his visit to Mauritius as a result of a police complaint made by the
16Defendant against him. He was provisionally charged with a case of swindling
17and an objection to departure was imposed upon him. The Plaintiff further avers
18that the acts and doings of the Defendant have caused more than trouble to
19him and he is hence praying for a judgment condemning and ordering the
20Defendant No.1 [sic] to pay to him the sum of Rs 2 million as damages.
211
22
232. The case was originally scheduled to be heard on the merits on the 20th of May
242024. However, the Defendant was absent and Learned Counsel for the
25Defendant was laid up. As the Plaintiff has travelled from the UK for the hearing
26of this case, the matter was postponed to the 22nd of May 2024. Counsel and
27Attorney for the Defendant were informed, through the representative of their
28chambers in court, that the case would be taken on the 22nd of May. On that
29day, despite several efforts made to secure the presence of Counsel for the
30Defendant, the latter never turned up in court. The case was maintained till 13
3140 hrs, at which time the instructing Attorney for the Defendant informed court
32that Counsel would not be attending court as he was before the District Court
33of Mahebourg. In these circumstances, the case was proceeded with in the
34absence of both the Defendant and Learned Counsel for the Defendant.
353. A plea dated the 19th of February 2019 was filed before the Supreme Court
36wherein the Defendant (then Defendant No.1) denied the claim and averred
37that he never committed any faute. The case was thereafter transferred from
38the Supreme Court to be heard before the Intermediate Court in 2022. The
39original proecipe was amended to remove Defendant Nos. 2 and 3 as parties,
40leaving only the Defendant No.1, now the Defendant, as the opponent. The plea
41of the Defendant was however never amended in the light of the amended
42proecipe.
434. On the 22nd of May 2024, the Plaintiff was called to depone under oath. He
44stated having caused a Notice of Trial to be served outside jurisdiction on the
45Defendant who also resides in England. The Defendant was touched personally
46as attested in an affidavit emanating from a UK process server and filed in court
47(Document A).
485. In a gist, the Plaintiff explained that he knew the Defendant since 2009 and that
49the latter made him believe that he could cure Plaintiff’s son. However, in 2012,
50the Defendant made a declaration to the police against the Plaintiff. The latter
51was subsequently arrested when he came to Mauritius on holidays in April 2014
52and a provisional plaint for swindling was lodged against him. The Plaintiff was
53bailed out but an objection to departure was imposed upon him. He had to retain
542
55
56the services of counsel to defend him and it was only in June 2014 that the
57provisional plaint was struck out and the Plaintiff was allowed to travel back to
58England.
596. The Plaintiff further stated that he suffered much prejudice due to this incident
60since his two weeks’ holidays with his family were ruined. His parents were very
61shocked and the family members thought him a thief. He had to fight very hard
62to clear his name as he did not commit that offence. Moreover, he could not
63report back to his job as scheduled after the two weeks since he was not
64allowed to travel and he nearly lost his job there. His autistic son and wife, who
65had travelled to Mauritius with him, had to go back to England on their own
66because his son needed medical support which was not available here and it
67was very hard for them. He also added that he was never prosecuted for the
68criminal case till today.
697. Certain documents were produced by the Plaintiff, namely a copy of the
70provisional plaint lodged against him together with a copy of the ruling striking
71out the provisional plaint (Document B) and a copy of the declaration made by
72the Defendant in 2012 (Document C). However, a perusal of the ruling at
73Document B shows that it in fact pertains to the refusal of the court to grant
74Plaintiff a variation of the Prohibition Order filed against him. Nevertheless,
75there is on record a letter from the police dated the 12th of June 2014 and
76addressed to the Plaintiff (Document E) wherein the latter was being informed
77that the provisional lodged against him before the District Court of Riviere du
78Rempart has been struck out. The Plaintiff also produced a notice “mise en
79demeure” dated the 2nd of December 2015 (Document D) which he caused to
80serve on several parties, including the Defendant.
818. Plaintiff added that this false allegation caused him a lot of trauma and he was
82too scared to come to Mauritius after that. He did not come to see his sick father
83nor did he come for his funeral and that remained his biggest regret. According
84to the Plaintiff, the Defendant went to make a declaration against him because
85the Plaintiff had previously reported the Defendant to the cybercrime unit for
86sending him messages. He stated that he never took money from the

90to heal his child.
919. I have duly considered the proecipe and the evidence adduced before me.
9210. Although the Defendant left default, this court has to ensure that any judgment
93given by it is grounded both in law and on the facts. Vide Hurnam v Bholah &
94anor 2009 SCJ 265.
9511. In essence, the case of the Plaintiff was that Defendant reported a false case
96of swindling against him which led to his arrest and inability to travel, hence
97causing him much prejudice. For such a claim to succeed, it is well established
98that the Plaintiff must be able to prove that the declaration was false and that it
99was made out of bad faith. In the case of Mohun v Jugnah & Anor (2002) SCJ
10036, the Supreme Court had this to say:-
101“Where a citizen suffers loss as a result of what is glaringly a criminal
102act it is clearly open to him to report the matter to the police for enquiry.
103It is also logical that he should impart to the police any information on
104the identity of the wrongdoer or suspected wrongdoer, depending
105upon the situation; unless the declarant is of bad faith, and the
106declaration is false and malicious, he stands no reason to fear any
107legal action from any person he has named and who may have been
108subjected to questioning by the Police. It is therefore essential, for his
109action to succeed, that the person who may have been subjected to
110unpleasant and perhaps strong questioning during a consequential
111police enquiry avers and proves “faute” on the part of the declarant.”
11212. A perusal of the proecipe shows that the Plaintiff never averred therein that the
113declaration made by the Defendant was false and made in bad faith. The
114proecipe therefore contains a “fatal deficiency” since the basis of tortious
115liability, and hence a valid cause of action, is clearly absent. As was held in the
116case of Mohun v Jugnah & Anor (supra), “since bad faith whilst making the
117declarations was not pleaded there was simply no cause of action against the
118appellant. The Learned Magistrate should not have taken pains to extricate
119from the evidence what was essential but missing in the plaint”.
1204
121
12213. I also wish to point out that pleading “faute lourde” cannot cure the above defect
123in the proecipe as “faute lourde” does not encompass the notion of bad faith. In
124the case of Securiclean (Mauritius) Ltd (under administration) v The
125District Council of Black River & Anor (2022) SCJ 139, the Supreme Court
126held that “faute lourde”, bad faith and “abus de droit” are separate and distinct
127notions. The relevant extract is as follows:-
128“It can also be gathered from the following passage, which was cited
129with approval by the Supreme Court in Souvenir Casting Ltd. &Ors
130v British Airways World Cargo [2008 SCJ 155] and which explains
131the distinction between “faute lourde” and “dol”, that “faute lourde” and
132“mauvaise foi” are mutually exclusive and that “faute lourde” is “non
133intentionnelle” and does not involve any “mauvaise foi” –
134“550. La considération de la faute lourde.
135(1) La faute lourde s’oppose au dol parce qu’elle est non intentionnelle.
136La distinction est rationnelle: le dol implique la mauvaise foi; la faute
137lourde, si énorme soit-elle, n’implique aucune mauvaise foi.…….. Or
138la faute lourde est une erreur, une négligence énorme, impardonnable,
139mais son auteur n’avait ni l’intention de nuire, ni même la
140connaissance du tort qui en résultera pour le créancier; il est encore
141de bonne foi. Bonne foi et mauvaise foi, telle est la difference
142fondamentale entre les deux notions …”(Underlining ours) (Droit Civil
143Les Obligations 5e Edition by François Terré, Philippe Simler et
144Yves Lequette at paragraph 550)
145….
146The submissions that bad faith is implicit in “faute lourde” and that the
147averment of “faute lourde” in the plaint encompasses bad faith, are
148untenable. In fact, although the “faute lourde” may be “une erreur, une
149negligence énorme, impardonnable” it is distinct from what is meant
150by bad faith which imports an intentional element.
1515
152
153In the circumstances we do not agree with counsel that in the present
154case bad faith can be inferred from the averment of “faute lourde”.”
15514. In any event, the testimony of the Plaintiff as well as the documents produced
156by him fell short of establishing that the declaration made by the Defendant was
157false and malicious. The Plaintiff merely stated that the declaration was false
158and that he never took money from the Defendant, without more. The fact that
159the provisional plaint lodged against the Plaintiff was struck out and that no
160prosecution has ensued so far do not amount to saying that the declaration of
161the Defendant was false. The Plaintiff still had to satisfy the court of the falsity
162of the declaration and the bad faith of the Defendant, which he failed to do.
16315. In the light of all the above, I therefore find that the Plaintiff has failed to
164establish, both in law and on facts, that the Defendant has committed a “faute”
165towards the Plaintiff. I accordingly dismiss the proecipe.
