90## 2a. Strict verbatim — the R15 contract for every quote-carrying field
91
92These fields are **strict-verbatim**: `citation_verbatim` (top-level, in `alt_citations[]`, and in `cases_cited[]`), `evidence_span`, `headnote.held[].quote`, `points_of_law[].quote`, and `foreign_doctrine[].reference`.
93
94For each of them: **copy the source text byte-exactly, INCLUDING OCR corruption, misspellings, and stray characters — never fix, normalize, modernize, or insert `[sic]`. The only permitted deviation is omission, marked with the canonical token `[...]`.**
95
96This is machine-enforced: validators split every strict-verbatim field on `[...]` and fragment-match each piece against the source lines (whitespace-collapse-only canonicalization). Any other alteration — a repaired digit, a deleted stray apostrophe, a corrected spelling, an inserted `[sic]`, a modernized accent — is a mismatch and **fails the run loudly**. There is no "obviously just OCR noise" exception; preservation is per-field-contract, not per-token judgment. One honest scoping exception: the pipeline's fragment matcher tolerates quote-glyph variants (curly vs straight apostrophes and quotation marks), so that one narrow class of alteration will not by itself fail the run — the behavioral prohibition still binds regardless; every other alteration named above fails loudly exactly as stated.

113## 2b. Line anchors — the citation-string-start rule (R11)
114
115This section governs `cases_cited[].line`, `legislation[].line`, and `foreign_doctrine[].line`. It fixes the #1 systemic defect of the baseline run: 9 wrapped-reference pointer errors, all from anchoring on the line carrying the *bulk* of a name or the citation number instead of the line where the reference *starts*.
116
117**What `citation_verbatim` captures:** the reference as printed at first mention, as **one contiguous span — from the first token of the case name through the last token of its citation number** (e.g. `Maloupe v The District Magistrate of Grand Port [2000] SCJ 233`). Surrounding prose ("the case of", "the authority of", "learned Counsel relied on") stays out. When no citation number is printed, the span is the case name alone (R12, §7).
118
119**The anchor rule:** `line` is, always and mechanically, **the printed line number on which that captured span's FIRST token sits.** A reference that wraps across lines — or across a page break — flows onward; its continuation lines are never the anchor. Never anchor on the line carrying the citation number, the bulk of the name, or a page-number line the reference wraps around.
120
121This also removes the baseline's internal inconsistency (Maloupe anchored at its cite-number line while Deelchand, in the same digest, was anchored at its name line): because the captured span begins at the name's first token, the anchor is the name-start line in every case — Maloupe 93, Deelchand 104, no convention to choose.

183## 5. Catchwords — legal-system prefix + broad→narrow chain + "Whether..." tail
184
185Every catchword is one em-dash-separated string. The **first** element is the legal-system prefix — there are **five** patterns:
186
187| Prefix | Use for |
188|---|---|
189| `Code Civil` | Substantive law from the Code Civil Mauricien (contract, tort/délictuelle, property, family, succession) |
190| `Code (<name>)` | Any **other** named code — Code de Procédure Civile, Code Pénal, Code de Commerce, etc. This generalized bucket exists specifically so procedure-code and non-CCM code matters have a home instead of being force-fit into `Code Civil`. |
191| `Common law` | Substantive English-derived law (negligence, natural justice, employment, evidence, equity) |
192| `Constitution` | Constitutional law / fundamental rights / separation of powers |
193| `Statute (<Act name>)` | A holding turning on a specific Act |
194
195Then subject → sub-area → (optional intermediate step) → and the **final** element should be a **"Whether..."** question capturing the point actually decided. Provisions cited (e.g. `Art. 806`, `s. 133`) sit just before the final "Whether..." element, not at the front.
196
197Examples:
198- `Code Civil — Contract — Art. 1108 — Consent — Whether duress vitiates consent`
199- `Code (Code de Procédure Civile) — Interlocutory relief — Art. 806 — Whether clear legal right, urgency and absence of serious bona fide defence shown`
200- `Common law — Negligence — Duty of care — Whether duty owed in tort independently of contract`
201- `Constitution — Separation of powers — Appointment of Chief Justice — ss. 77(1) and 64(5) — Whether the courts may stay the President's consultation process`
202- `Statute (Road Traffic Act) — Disqualification — s. 133 — Whether disqualification mandatory on first conviction`
203
204**Quality gate:** if you genuinely cannot produce a clean "Whether..." final element for a substantive holding, the case probably lacks a clean ratio for that point. Do not force one. Set `confidence.catchwords: "low"`, add a `notes` entry explaining why, and consider setting top-level `needs_human_review: true`.

Before finalizing `catchwords`, walk your own `headnote.held[]` list and confirm each entry has a home. A Held proposition with no matching catchword chain is a sign you've under-covered the judgment, not a sign that entry didn't need one — this was the single most common Tier-2-into-Tier-1 gap in verified test output.

208## 6. Held and disposition
209
210- `headnote.held[]` — **numbered atomic propositions**, each a rule, never a recital of who won.
211  - Right: *"A guarantor's liability under s. 10 is discharged where the principal contract is materially varied without consent."*
212  - Wrong: *"The court held in favour of the appellant."*
213  - **What makes a proposition a holding — apply before drafting the entry, not after:** (i) *Necessity* — reverse the proposition's meaning and re-read the disposition as actually written; if the disposition would need to change, the proposition was necessary to it (Wambaugh's inversion test). Cross's own formulation asks the same necessity question more narrowly: whether the proposition is necessary given the material facts within the court's actual reasoning — not a broader hypothetical class of facts the court never reached. (ii) *Facts-basis* — the proposition rests on the specific facts the court had before it, not a hypothetical the court merely floated. (iii) *Narrowness* — state it at the narrowest formulation the facts actually required; do not broaden a fact-specific rule into a general principle the court had no need to reach. This constrains YOUR inference beyond what the court needed — it is not license to artificially narrow a rule the court itself states broadly: where the court's own operative rule is a validly broad règle de droit (the civilian/Cassation-style formulation common when a Mauritian judgment disposes of a matter via the Code Civil or French doctrine), record it at the breadth the court itself gave it. Use the court's own introduction language as a cheap first signal, never as the final word: "we hold", "accordingly", "it follows that" point toward a holding; "we note", "it may be that", "in passing" point toward dictum — but (i)–(iii) govern whenever the language itself is ambiguous or absent, which is common in terse Mauritian dispositions.
214  - Each proposition carries `pinpoints[]` — **one or more** `{start_line, end_line}` ranges pointing at the supporting passage(s). Use a single-line range (`start_line == end_line`) when the point sits on one line; use multiple ranges when the reasoning for one proposition is spread across separate passages (e.g. the court states the general rule in one place and applies it to the facts elsewhere) — do not compress multiple genuinely separate supporting passages into one artificially wide range.
215  - **Each proposition carries `quote` — REQUIRED (R16):** a verbatim excerpt, taken from **within that entry's own pinpointed passage(s)**, grounding the proposition in the court's own words. Strict-verbatim, §2a. When the supporting reasoning is dispersed across pinpoints, assemble the quote from pieces joined by `[...]` — each piece must still sit inside a pinpointed range. Never quote from outside the pinpoints, and never compose or paraphrase inside this field. **Quote-within-pinpoints self-check (v2.2):** self-check every quote against its own pinpoints before finalizing: if the quote naturally needs text outside the declared `start_line`/`end_line`, either widen the pinpoint range to cover it, or split into an additional pinpoint entry and join the quote pieces with `[...]` per §2a — never leave a quote that reaches outside what its own pinpoints declare.
216- **`quotable_holding` no longer exists.** It was removed from the schema in v2; its role is fully replaced by the per-entry `held[].quote`. Do not emit it anywhere.
217- `headnote.disposition` — operative verbs only. Right: *"Appeal allowed; conviction quashed; matter remitted for retrial."* Wrong: *"The court ruled in the appellant's favour."*
218- `headnote.orders[]` — populate only when there are explicit orders (costs, directions) distinct from the disposition sentence itself.
219- `headnote.facts` — 3–5 sentences, third person, past tense, neutral. Refer to parties by role ("the applicant", "the respondent") unless role alone is ambiguous. Don't editorialize. **Per-party accuracy:** when the facts involve multiple parties, defendants, or companies, keep each one's role, amount, or allegation distinct as the text actually states it — do not smooth two co-defendants or two companies into one uniform sentence, and do not carry a fact stated for one party over to another party in the same passage just because they're mentioned together. **Epistemic discipline (baseline defect):** keep the source's epistemic qualifiers and exact counts. "5.71 grams of *suspected* synthetic cannabinoids" must never become "5.71 grams of synthetic cannabinoids" — dropping "suspected" in a bail digest asserts an unproven fact against the applicant. One previous conviction is never "prior convictions"; "borne on record for six drug related offences" is a record count, not six convictions. Allegations stay allegations, suspicions stay suspicions, and counts stay exact — in `facts` and in every composed field that touches them.

221## 6a. Points of law — the non-decisive points-of-law layer (R19) and the candidate ledger (R23)
222
223New in v2: a top-level array `points_of_law[]`, **required, may be empty**. Together with `headnote.held[]` it must capture **every point of law the judgment states or applies** — each point going in **exactly one** of the two buckets:
224
225- **Decisive of an issue in the case → `headnote.held[]`** (all §6 rules unchanged).
226- **Stated or applied, but NOT decisive → `points_of_law[]`.**
227
228**Completeness pass — run this FIRST, before the boundary test below.** Read the court's reasoning once specifically hunting for every legal proposition it states, applies, or relies on, independent of whether you have already found a quote for it; build a working list of candidates before sorting any of them — that working list IS the `legal_point_candidates[]` array (R23, next paragraph), one and the same artifact, never a separate private scratch list. Then run the boundary test, the carve-out, and the overlap check further below to sort each candidate into `held[]` or `points_of_law[]`, and only then attach pinpoints and quotes. A proposition being absent from both lists is usually not a misclassification — it is a proposition the extraction never surfaced at all, which is why this needs its own pass rather than being folded into the classify-and-quote step. Note also: a rule captured in a `cited_for` gloss on an authority is not thereby captured here — if this judgment itself adopts or applies that same rule, it still needs its own `held[]`/`points_of_law[]` entry. When a candidate legal point traces to a named treatise, code commentary, or secondary doctrinal source (Archbold, Halsbury, Garraud, Terré, Dalloz, etc.), confirm that source also has its own `foreign_doctrine[]` entry (§8) — a point captured in `held[]` or `points_of_law[]` with its supporting doctrine silently dropped is the same completeness failure as an omitted holding, one field over.
229
230**R23 — the candidate list is now an output field, not private reasoning.** Populate the new **required top-level array `legal_point_candidates[]`** (minItems 1 — every judgment has at least one legal point) with every candidate you identify during this pass, in the order you find them. Each entry carries: `description` — a short composed phrase naming the candidate proposition (composed, R15-exempt per §2a, never fragment-matched; it identifies the candidate and need not be full `held[]`-quality prose); `source_pinpoint` — one `{start_line, end_line}` range recording where you found it, printed-line-number discipline as everywhere else; `disposition` — exactly one of `"held"`, `"points_of_law"`, `"excluded"`; plus `disposition_ref`, `exclusion_reason`, and `duplicate_of_index` as governed below. As you sort each candidate into `held[]` or `points_of_law[]`, set its `disposition` and its `disposition_ref` to the 0-based index it landed at in that array — and make sure its `source_pinpoint` overlaps at least one of the `pinpoints` ranges on the entry it became (a validator cross-checks index-trace and pinpoint overlap mechanically). Bookkeeping note: ledger entries are written in the order you find candidates, but `disposition_ref` and the exclusion fields are finalized AFTER the `held[]`/`points_of_law[]` arrays are themselves final — revisiting those fields at that point is normal and does not contradict the anti-backfill rule below, which forbids inventing candidates post-hoc, not updating fields on candidates you already recorded. `disposition_ref` is null when — and only when — the candidate is excluded. A candidate that doesn't qualify for either list gets `disposition: "excluded"` and a specific `exclusion_reason` — never silently drop a candidate you already identified. `exclusion_reason` is a closed vocabulary operationalizing the "what does NOT qualify" criteria below: `not_adopted_by_court` | `pure_fact_finding` | `background_recital_not_applied` | `duplicate_of_candidate` | `hypothetical_arguendo` | `not_reached_left_undecided` | `other`; it is non-null when — and only when — the candidate is excluded. `not_reached_left_undecided` covers a point the court expressly declines to decide (jurisdiction found lacking, the point moot, "we do not reach the merits") — distinct from `not_adopted_by_court`, where the court engages with a proposition and does not adopt it: here the court neither adopts nor rejects, it declines to reach the point at all.
231
232**Negative example — do not backfill the ledger from the finished lists.** A ledger with exactly as many entries as `held[]` + `points_of_law[]` combined, every one mapped 1:1 with zero `excluded` entries, is not evidence of a genuine hunt — it is what the ledger looks like when built backward from the already-finished lists. Any judgment with more than a handful of lines of substantive reasoning will surface at least one candidate that doesn't survive: a party's submission the court doesn't adopt, background law recited but not applied to these facts, a finding of fact mistakenly rule-shaped, a hypothetical the court merely assumes. If your ledger has zero exclusions, re-read the reasoning once more before finalizing — you likely wrote the ledger after deciding the lists, not before.
233
234**Two mechanical sub-rules for the ledger fields.** When `exclusion_reason` is `"other"` (none of the six named categories fit), you MUST also add a dedicated `notes[]` entry (per R21, §12: pin the line) that names the candidate by its `source_pinpoint` line and states the specific reason — `"other"` is not a silent catch-all. When `exclusion_reason` is `"duplicate_of_candidate"`, set `duplicate_of_index` to the 0-based index of the earlier candidate in this same array that it duplicates — never leave it null when the reason is duplication, and never point it at an index that comes after the current one.
235
236**Worked example — a necessary premise is its own held[] entry, not background.** 1953 MR 15's holding on partial drunkenness (that it cannot be left to the jury on intent to kill) rests on a prior, threshold proposition: that Mauritian provisions borrowed from French law are construed by French doctrine and jurisprudence, not English. Run the §6 necessity test on the threshold proposition itself, independent of the substantive rule it enables: strike it, and the court's basis for adopting the French complete/partial-drunkenness distinction collapses — the substantive holding would need a different foundation entirely. A methodological or interpretive premise the court adopts specifically to reach its substantive rule is itself necessary to the disposition and belongs in `held[]`, even though it reads like background law rather than a case-specific ruling.
237
238**The boundary test — run it per point:** *strike the proposition from the judgment and re-read the disposition. If any operative part of the disposition no longer follows — the court could not have reached this outcome without this point — the point is decisive: it goes in `held[]`. If the disposition still follows exactly as written, the court stated or applied the point along the way but did not rest the outcome on it: it goes in `points_of_law[]`.* Cross-check: `held[]` answers "why did this side win or lose?"; `points_of_law[]` answers "what law does this judgment state that a researcher could cite it for, beyond the winning reasons?"
239
240The two lists are **disjoint** — never place the same proposition (or a reworded twin) in both. When the deletion test leaves you genuinely torn, prefer `held[]` and flag the doubt in `notes[]` with `confidence.points_of_law` set accordingly.
241
242**Alternative-holdings carve-out.** The deletion test misfires when the court gives two or more independently sufficient grounds for the same disposition — striking any single ground alone leaves the disposition intact, which would wrongly push every ground but one into `points_of_law[]`. When the judgment states multiple independent grounds each of which alone would support the disposition, each is its own `held[]` entry — do not merge them and do not demote a later or alternatively-framed ground to `points_of_law[]` merely because the court reached it second, called it "in the alternative," or because another ground would have sufficed on its own. **Arguendo exclusion:** this carve-out reaches only grounds the court actually DECIDES — a ground the court merely assumes against itself for the sake of argument ("even if...", "assuming without deciding...") is hypothetical, not an independent holding, and belongs in `points_of_law[]` if it belongs anywhere at all. Negative example: "even assuming locus standi (which we doubt), the claim fails on the merits" states only one decided ground — the merits ruling is `held[]`; the assumed-arguendo locus standi point is not. Ledger tiebreak: a merely-assumed premise ("assuming without deciding...") is `excluded: hypothetical_arguendo` in `legal_point_candidates[]`; only an arguendo point the court actually articulates and applies as a rule reaches `points_of_law[]`.
243
244What qualifies as "stated or applied": the **court's own** statements — settled rules restated en route, legal tests recited from authorities and endorsed or used, procedural or evidential rules applied without controversy, obiter observations of rule form. What does NOT qualify: counsel's propositions merely reported and never adopted; propositions that appear only inside quoted material the court does not adopt or use; pure findings of fact.
245
246**Consequential-rule vs. order (v2.2).** A rule explaining *why* a consequential order follows from the primary disposition (e.g. "licence endorsement falls automatically when the underlying disqualification is set aside") is a `held[]`/`points_of_law[]` candidate in its own right, sorted by the same boundary test as any other point — it is not satisfied by recording the resulting order alone in `headnote.orders[]`. If the court states the rule, capture the rule; if it also issues the order, `orders[]` records that separately.
247
248**Working gloss:** `held[]` is the ratio — the point(s) of law the case actually turns on. Decisiveness alone governs this list, regardless of how reportable or mundane the point is. `points_of_law[]` is everything else the judgment states or applies but that did not, on its own, decide the outcome — including points a professional headnote would flag as reportable precisely because they are NOT the ratio: a weighty obiter observation is reportable yet never `held[]`; a routine, undramatic ratio is `held[]` even when no editor would think it worth reporting on its own.
249
250**Overlap check — run before finalizing either list:** compare every `points_of_law[]` entry against every `held[]` entry. Merge two entries into one — keep the decisive proposition in `held[]`, drop the `points_of_law[]` duplicate — **only when the proposition itself restates the same rule.** Overlapping `pinpoints` or an overlapping `quote` between a `points_of_law[]` entry and a `held[]` entry is a **flag to re-examine, never on its own a reason to merge**: a single dense passage routinely states two distinct rules at once (2024 INT 180 para 13 states both "faute lourde ≠ bad faith" and the court's separate three-notion distinction in the same lines — same pinpoints, two propositions, both entries stand).
251
252Each `points_of_law[]` entry:
253- `proposition` — ONE composed rule-form sentence (same composition standard as `held[].proposition`; not verbatim, R15 does not apply).
254- `pinpoints[]` — **mandatory**: one or more `{start_line, end_line}` ranges where the point is stated or applied, same printed-line-number discipline as everywhere else.
255- `quote` — **optional**: a verbatim excerpt (strict-verbatim, §2a) only when a clean supporting passage exists within the pinpointed range(s). If none does, **omit the key entirely** — never force, compose, or approximate one. **Quote-within-pinpoints self-check (v2.2):** self-check every quote against its own pinpoints before finalizing: if the quote naturally needs text outside the declared `start_line`/`end_line`, either widen the pinpoint range to cover it, or split into an additional pinpoint entry and join the quote pieces with `[...]` per §2a — never leave a quote that reaches outside what its own pinpoints declare.
256- `provenance` — **REQUIRED (R24, v2.2)**: `court_reasoning | party_argument` — see the R24 rule immediately below.
257
258**R24 — provenance is required on every `points_of_law[]` entry.** Run the same decision procedure as §7.3: locate the sentence that first introduces the point, identify its grammatical subject. Subject is counsel or a party → `party_argument`, even where the court goes on to restate or apply the point at length. Subject is the court itself → `court_reasoning`. This tag does not reopen whether the point qualifies for `points_of_law[]` at all — a point whose provenance is `party_argument` still had to pass the existing "stated or applied" boundary test to be here in the first place; this only marks that its origin is the party's framing, so a downstream reader does not mistake a counsel-sourced proposition the court merely engaged with for a rule the court itself pronounced.
259
260**Naming warning — read twice:** `headnote` (singular) is the existing OBJECT holding `facts` / `held` / `disposition` / `orders`; `points_of_law` (plural array) is this new TOP-LEVEL ARRAY of non-decisive points. The rename from `headnotes` to `points_of_law` (2026-07-16) removed the name collision between the two — they remain different fields at different places in the schema; never merge them, nest one inside the other, or move content between them. And `legal_point_candidates[]` (R23, above) is a third, separate top-level array — the pre-sort ledger; it never replaces or absorbs either of the other two.

262## 7. Cases cited — the citation-graph spine
263
264Every entry in `cases_cited[]` needs **all** of: `citation` (normalized — **null, with the key present, when the source prints no citation number at all**), `citation_verbatim` (the captured reference span, §2b — strict-verbatim, §2a), `name`, `cited_for` (§7.5), `treatment`, `signal`, `review_worthy`, `provenance`, `line` (the §2b anchor — a single integer, first mention only, even if the case is cited again later), and `evidence_span` (a short verbatim quote from the judgment that justifies the treatment you assigned — the reviewer's trust anchor, strict-verbatim per §2a; may be `null` only for bare string-cites in a list where no case-specific language exists beyond the list itself).
265
266**No-citation cases (R12):** when the source names a case with no citation number, set `citation: null` — the key stays present; a renderer will display "none". **Never a placeholder string.** The v1 sentinel `"(no citation in source)"` is abolished and must not appear anywhere in your output: the sentinel literal never appears in the source text, so the validator's VERBATIM fragment-match gate flags it as a FRAGMENT MISMATCH the moment you emit it; the schema's `not`/`const` guard on this field is documentation only — the validator does not execute `not`. `citation_verbatim` is then the case name as printed (§2b), and `name` is still filled. R20 (§3) extends this same mechanical pattern to normalization: when a citation number **is** printed but only as a report-series reference you cannot normalize from the printed text alone, `citation` is null too — never a court-code+number pair supplied from your own knowledge.
267
268**`name` is required on every edge** — it is the key for downstream local name-matching that runs with no LLM in the loop, so capture it even when the citation is perfect and the name feels redundant. If the source identifies a decision only by reporter reference with no party names, see §7.6. `name` is also governed by R26 (§3): record the relationship the source actually presents — never recast parties the source presents as joint, co-obligor, or otherwise non-adversarial into an invented adversarial "X v Y" rendering.
269
270**`evidence_lines` (optional):** a `{start_line, end_line}` range bounding where `evidence_span` sits in the source. Fill it whenever the span is **not** at or immediately adjacent to the edge's `line` anchor (typical when the court's treatment verdict lands pages after the first mention); omit it when the span sits at or next to the anchor. Validators use it as the fragment-match window.
271
272**Completeness — every authority the court relies on is an edge, including in pre-modern documents.** Older MR-series judgments cite authorities in archaic layouts: report-series references ("(1866) Law Reports, Privy Council Appeals Vol. 1 p. 169"), no neutral citations, names buried mid-sentence in long French or English passages. They still count. The baseline missed *Bruneau* and *Sérendat* — the two authorities the 1953 judgment cited for the very rule of construction the entire judgment applies. An archaic citation format is never a reason to drop an edge; the authority for the operative rule is the last edge you may drop.
273
274**Pinpoint self-check:** before recording `line`, or any `start_line`/`end_line` anywhere in the schema, re-locate the printed line-number prefix (§1) at the exact point the captured span or passage actually begins — do not estimate, round, or carry over a nearby number from an earlier field. Anchors follow §2b: the first token's line, especially when the reference wraps.

276### 7.1 Treatment — exactly 7 verbs
277
278`applied | considered | distinguished | not followed | overruled | referred to | undetermined`
279
280**Serialization warning — mechanical, validation-breaking:** the verbs are enum values containing **SPACES**: `"referred to"`, `"not followed"` — **never underscores**. `referred_to` is not a valid value; a baseline run emitted it on all 20 neutral edges of a single case and failed schema validation outright. If your internal habit is snake_case, override it in this field.
281
282| Verb            | Means                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
283| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
284| `applied`       | The court used the cited case's holding to decide a parallel issue in *this* judgment                                                                                                                                           |
285| `considered`    | **STRICT rule:** the court itself interpretively engages with what the cited case *means* — explains it, works through its reasoning, extends or reconciles it — not merely notes that it exists                                |
286| `distinguished` | The court found the cited case factually or doctrinally different from the present case                                                                                                                                         |
287| `not followed`  | The court explicitly declined to apply an authority that looked applicable                                                                                                                                                      |
288| `overruled`     | The court declared the cited case wrongly decided (rare — almost always full-bench/en banc)                                                                                                                                     |
289| `referred to`   | The citation is named/mentioned but receives **no** independent interpretive engagement — string-cited lists, background litigation history, "counsel relied on X", a quoted passage that merely reproduces another case's text |
290| `undetermined`  | You cannot tell **which** of the other six verbs applies at all — the text is too garbled, truncated, or genuinely ambiguous about the nature of the citation                                                                   |
291
292**The STRICT `considered` test, worked:**
293- *Considered*: "the principle in [case A] was reiterated in [case B], and the court examined how each element applied to disciplinary hearings" → the court is doing its own interpretive work.
294- *Referred to*, not considered: "Learned Counsel for the applicant relied on the case of [X]... damages and not an injunction is the proper remedy" → this is counsel's citation, reproduced, with no independent court engagement. `provenance: party_argument`.
295- A long block-quote reproducing another judgment's reasoning verbatim, with no commentary of the court's own, is `referred to`, however long the quote is.
296
297**`referred to` is not a symptom of uncertainty — it is a confident, correct classification for unengaged mentions.** Do not escalate an unengaged string-cite to `undetermined` just because it's terse.
298
299**`undetermined` is the mandatory default only for genuine verb-ambiguity** — when you cannot tell whether the court even mentioned, engaged with, or rejected the citation's reasoning. **Never** silently launder that genuine uncertainty into `referred to` as a safe-sounding guess — that tells a downstream lawyer a precedent is fine when its status is actually unknown, which is the single most dangerous error this pipeline can make.
300
301**Separately, a graduated-caution rule** for when you *can* see engagement but are unsure of its strength: when genuinely unsure between `applied` and `considered`, prefer `considered`. When unsure between `considered` and `referred to`, prefer `referred to`. This only applies when you have some signal — it does not override the `undetermined` rule above for total ambiguity.
302
303**`reversed` is never a `cases_cited` treatment verb.** If the judgment states that this case is itself an appeal from, or was itself reversed on appeal from, another decision in the **same litigation**, that is procedural history, not a citation to an authority — it belongs in `case_history_chain[]` (§13), never in `cases_cited[]`.

305### 7.1a Citation Treatment Guide — a decision procedure, not just definitions
306
307*Introduced in v1.1; verified at 39/39 correct treatment verbs on unseen cases in the 2026-07-15 round — apply it exactly as written.* The definitions in §7.1 are correct, but under pressure they don't disambiguate reliably: an earlier verified test round found 5 of 29 treatment calls wrong, all on the considered/distinguished/applied boundary. This guide adds a **sequence** to run per cited case on top of those definitions — it does not replace or weaken the STRICT `considered` rule or the `undetermined`-never-laundered rule above.
308
309**First, read every mention of the case to the end.** A judgment often engages with a cited case in one passage and delivers its actual verdict on that case — applied, distinguished, not followed — in a separate, later passage. Anchoring on the first passage you find is the most common way this ontology gets misapplied.
310
311**Then run these tests in order; stop at the first one that fires:**
312
3131. **Applied?** Is the cited case's rule, test, or checklist actually operationalized against these facts to reach a holding or `headnote.held[]` proposition in *this* judgment — not merely explained, but *used to decide*? → `applied`.
3142. **Distinguished?** Does the court say, in its own words, that *this cited case* is factually or doctrinally different from, or does not govern, the present matter? → `distinguished` (+ `review_worthy: true`).
3153. **Not followed / overruled?** Does the court expressly decline to apply an authority that looked applicable, or declare it wrongly decided? → `not followed` / `overruled`.
3164. **Considered (STRICT)?** Does the court do its own interpretive work on what the cited case means — explains it, works through its reasoning, extends or reconciles it, or examines/quotes its reasoning to help resolve a *different* point — without adopting it as the operative rule (step 1) and without declaring it inapplicable (step 2)? → `considered`.
3175. **Referred to?** Named or mentioned with no independent engagement — string-cite, background, counsel's argument reported but not adopted, a quote reproduced with no court commentary *and not used to decide anything*? → `referred to`.
3186. **Undetermined?** After steps 1–5 you genuinely cannot tell what the court did with this citation — too garbled, truncated, or ambiguous about the nature of the mention itself? → `undetermined`. Never fall back to `referred to` here just to sound confident.
319
320**Two traps, named:**
321
322- **Topical proximity is not distinguishing.** A distinguish-flavored word (*distinguish, differentiate, unlike*) appearing near a citation does not mean the court distinguished *that cited case*. If the sentence is doing the cited case's *own* internal reasoning — e.g. the cited case itself differentiates between two statutory offences — that's step 4 (`considered`), not step 2. Step 2 fires only when the court's own words state the relationship *between the cited case and the present matter*.
323- **Quote-without-commentary is `referred to` — unless the quote is the decision.** Reproducing a passage from a cited case with no interpretive comment of the court's own is `referred to` (per §7.1). But if that same quoted passage is then the rule the court uses to decide — i.e. it becomes, or is, a `headnote.held[]` proposition — step 1 controls and the verb is `applied`, regardless of how bare the surrounding prose is.
324
325**Worked examples, from real misclassifications (2026-07-05 verification):**
326
327- *Hems Apparels v State Bank of Mauritius* 2009 SCJ 419: the court says it will "list out and summarise" Hems Apparels' principles on amending pleadings, then runs them against the facts of the motion before it. Wrongly called `considered`; correct is `applied` — the checklist is the operative test, not just explained.
328- *I. Jangeerkhan v The State* 1997 SCJ 133: an early passage reads as interpretive engagement (what the court found on Jangeerkhan's facts); a later, separate passage states outright "the cases of Ramoly 1987 and Jangeerkhan can be distinguished from the present matter". Wrongly called `considered` (anchored on the early passage, missed the later verdict); correct is `distinguished` + `review_worthy: true`.
329- *Rughooputh v State* 1997 SCJ 329 and *DPP v Iframac* 1999 SCJ 9: both passages recount the cited case's *own* internal distinction between two statutory offences, quoted to help interpret a different charge — the court never says either case is inapplicable here. Wrongly called `distinguished` (triggered by "differentiated" appearing in the sentence); correct is `considered`.
330- *Nundoosingh v Standard Bank* 2018 SCJ 122: quoted verbatim on the Judge in Chambers' jurisdiction, with no visible court commentary — but that exact quoted proposition becomes this judgment's Held #4. Wrongly called `referred to`; correct is `applied` (the quote-without-commentary trap, resolved by the quote-becomes-decision exception above).
331
332**Contrast — the same boundary, correctly called:**
333
334- *Karuthasami v Equilibre Ltd* 2013 SCJ 338: "Learned Counsel for the applicant relied on the case of..." — reported argument, never adopted. `referred to` / `party_argument`.
335- *Ramsewak v MBC* 1994 SCJ 297: the court states the objection "cannot be invoked at this stage," deciding the point directly on Ramsewak's authority. `applied`.
336- *Ramoly v State* 1987 MR 29: the very sentence that correctly distinguishes Jangeerkhan (above) also names Ramoly — "can be distinguished from the present matter inasmuch as..." — showing one sentence can carry the same verdict for two cited cases named together.
337
338**Positive/neutral boundary — additional cross-check (v2.1 addition; does not replace or reorder steps 1–6 above, run it after them as a verification pass on `applied`/`considered`/`referred to` calls).** Two industry-calibrated questions:
3391. *Followed-test:* does the reliance language attach to the CITED CASE ITSELF — not to a nearby principle, rule, or statute the court happens to be following — as the basis for deciding here? e.g. "applying the test in [case]", or "bound to hold, following [case]'s construction of the article." If the case itself is what the court says it is relying on, `applied`, even where the surrounding language is terse or the court never runs a numbered checklist. If the court only explains, expands, or interprets the case without adopting it as that basis, `considered`.
3402. *Cited-test:* delete every word that is the court's own commentary from the passage — if what remains is only the case's name, citation, and a reproduced quote with no interpretive addition, the verb is `referred to`, not `considered`, however long the reproduced material is. Do not apply `considered` on length or elaborateness alone. **Exception:** this test never overrides step 1 — if that same reproduced quote is itself, or becomes, a `headnote.held[]` proposition, the verb is `applied` regardless of how bare the surrounding commentary is (the quote-becomes-decision exception already named in §7.1a's traps above; *Nundoosingh*).
341
342**Worked negative example, teaching both halves of the followed-test:** in 1953 MR 15, "we are consequently bound to hold, following those principles" (src 693–694) attaches grammatically to the French substantive principles the passage has just stated — not to *Bruneau* or *Sérendat*, named roughly 35 lines earlier. That phrase alone does not upgrade Bruneau/Sérendat to `applied`. They earn `applied` anyway, but for a different reason entirely: the court adopts their construction rule — the specific interpretive method those two cases establish — as its own operative method for resolving this case, which is step 1 of the decision procedure above, not proximity to reliance language. Generalize from this: express-sounding reliance language sitting near a citation does not by itself upgrade that citation; only the court actually adopting the cited case's own rule, test, or method as its operative basis does.
343
344**Precedence, when the two applied-triggering tests could conflict:** when the followed-test above fires — reliance language genuinely attaches to the cited case itself as the basis for this decision — it overrides an earlier, tentative `considered` or `referred to` call reached before you ran this cross-check; reclassify to `applied`. §7.1's graduated-caution rule ("when unsure between `applied` and `considered`, prefer `considered`") governs only the cases where the followed-test does NOT fire — it is not a reason to skip the followed-test or to keep a tentative `considered` call once the followed-test resolves it.
345
346**R22 — evidence-span self-sufficiency (v2.2 addition; run last, after §7.1a's boundary cross-check above).** Before finalizing any engagement-verb treatment (`applied`, `considered`, `distinguished`, `not followed`, `overruled`), or any treatment of any kind carrying a non-null `evidence_span`, reread ONLY the `evidence_span` you are about to record — not the surrounding passage, not your own reasoning about it — and ask: if a reviewer saw nothing but this quoted span, would it demonstrate the verb you assigned? A span that merely names the cited case, or sits near language about a *different* case or a different distinction, does not demonstrate `distinguished`; a span that shows the court analogizing to or explaining the cited case's reasoning without adopting it as the operative rule does not demonstrate `applied`. If the span you would cite doesn't carry the verb on its own, the verb is unsupported — widen the span until it does, or step back to `undetermined` — never a one-step downgrade to a safer-sounding verb such as `referred to` — per the existing `undetermined`-never-laundered rule (§7.1). Exemption: a bare string-cite `referred to` edge whose `evidence_span` is legitimately `null` under §7 (no case-specific language beyond the list itself) is outside this check — do NOT flip it to `undetermined` for lacking a span, and do NOT fabricate a span to satisfy this rule. This applies identically to `cited_for` (§7.5) and to any engagement claim written into `notes[]` (R21, §12): no engagement verb, anywhere in the output, without a pinpointed span that shows it on its own terms.

348### 7.2 Signal bucket — mechanical lookup, don't re-derive it
349
350| `treatment` | `signal` |
351|---|---|
352| `applied` | `positive` |
353| `considered` | `neutral` |
354| `referred to` | `neutral` |
355| `distinguished` | `neutral` — **always** pair with `review_worthy: true` (Shepard's convention: distinguishing is neutral, not negative, but it is the single most contested mapping — the flag exists precisely to compensate) |
356| `not followed` | `caution` |
357| `overruled` | `negative` |
358| `undetermined` | `neutral` (grey — the `treatment` field itself already signals the uncertainty; don't also mark it caution or negative) |
359
360`review_worthy` is a boolean: **must be `true` whenever `treatment` is `distinguished`**; may be `true` for any other entry where you have genuine doubt about the classification; `false` otherwise.
361
362### 7.3 Provenance
363
364`court_reasoning | party_argument | background | nested` — required on every entry.
365- `court_reasoning` — the court itself cites/engages with the authority in its own reasoning.
366- `party_argument` — counsel cited it; the court is reporting counsel's argument, not (yet) adopting it.
367- `background` — cited as background/context (e.g. a party's litigation history, a general string-cite list) rather than for its holding.
368- `nested` — the citation appears only inside a block quote reproduced from a *different* cited authority (i.e. this judgment didn't independently cite it — the case it quoted did). Note which outer citation it is nested inside in `notes[]` if it materially matters.
369
370**Provenance decision procedure.** Locate the sentence that first introduces the cited authority — not the sentence carrying the eventual treatment verdict, which may be pages later — and identify its grammatical subject. Subject is a party or counsel ("Learned Counsel for the respondent relied on...", "It is the contention of the applicant that...", "[X] referred to...", "[X] quoted...") → `party_argument`, even where the court goes on to reproduce or restate the same material at length. Subject is the court itself ("The court finds...", "This principle was reiterated by us...") → `court_reasoning`. **Cue-phrase trap:** a phrase like "the court considers that" or "the court held" appearing inside a passage reporting a party's submission describes the *cited authority's own* court, not this one — trace back to the actual introducing subject before assigning provenance; never classify from the cue phrase in isolation.
371
372### 7.4 `authority_observed` — only when the text speaks
373
374Optional per entry: `authority_observed: "binding" | "persuasive"` + `authority_note` (short paraphrase of the cue). Populate **only** when the judgment explicitly comments on the cited case's authority status — e.g. "we are bound by [JCPC case]" → `binding`; "this English authority is persuasive only" → `persuasive`. When the judgment is silent, omit **both** fields entirely. Never infer authority from citation format alone (that inference is computed downstream, not by you).
375
376### 7.5 `cited_for` — the proposition this edge stands for (R18)
377
378**Required on every edge:** ONE composed sentence stating the legal proposition for which THIS case is cited in THIS judgment. Ground it **strictly in this edge's `evidence_span` and the source text at/around its `line` anchor** — never in anything you believe you know about the cited case from outside this judgment (§2: you know nothing). Composed, not verbatim — R15 does not apply and this field is never fragment-matched; do not just copy the evidence_span into it.
379
380Shape: proposition-form, one sentence. Right: *"Cited for the proposition that bail may be refused where no conditions can render the risk of reoffending negligible."* Wrong: *"The court referred to this case"* (treatment restatement, no proposition), or a proposition sourced from your own memory of the cited case rather than from what this judgment uses it for. Downstream, this sentence is the join key toward the cited case's own `held[]`/`points_of_law[]` propositions, so it must reflect what *this* judgment took from the authority — nothing more.
381
382### 7.6 Foreign authorities stay in `cases_cited` (R13)
383
384Foreign cases are edges in the same array — never a separate bucket, never demoted to `foreign_doctrine`. Mark them with three optional fields:
385
386- `jurisdiction` — lowercase code, written **ONLY for non-Mauritian authorities** (`"uk"` incl. Privy Council and English courts, `"fr"`, `"in"`, `"za"`, ...). **For Mauritian authorities, OMIT the key entirely** — downstream reads an absent key as `"mu"`. Never infer a jurisdiction beyond what the citation form or the judgment text supports. **Supranational tribunals (v2.2):** for decisions of a supranational or international tribunal (e.g. the European Court of Human Rights), use `"echr"` (or the tribunal's own natural code) — never `"uk"` merely because the citation or party names read as European or English-language. Pair with `court` naming the tribunal (e.g. `"European Court of Human Rights"`) so the two fields agree.
387- `court` — the deciding court, when stated or evident from the citation form (e.g. `"Privy Council"`, `"Court of Appeal of England and Wales"`, `"Cour de Cassation"`). Omit when not evident.
388- `source_report` — the law-report volume/page reference as printed, when the case is cited from a report series (e.g. `"(1866) Law Reports, Privy Council Appeals (1865–1867) Vol. 1 p. 169"`). Omit when the source gives none.
389
390**French Cour de Cassation decisions cited in the court's own reasoning ARE `cases_cited` edges** — even when identified only by bulletin or recueil number. The baseline dropped three such authorities from the 1953 judgment (e.g. *"Bulletins des Arrêts de la Cour de Cassation 1868 No. 196"*, which the court quoted directly). For a **nameless, reporter-only cite** like that: record the reporter reference as printed in `citation_verbatim` (and in `citation`, unnormalized — French citations keep their own convention, §3); put the reporter designation as printed in `name`; set `review_worthy: true` so a human can weigh the graph value of an unresolvable 19th-century cite. `jurisdiction: "fr"`, `court` when evident.

396## 8. Legislation cited
397
398Every entry needs `kind`, `name`, `jurisdiction`, and `line` (first-mention anchor per §2b — the line where the instrument's name starts; the Bail Act example there is a real baseline error). `sections[]` when specific provisions are cited.
399
400| `kind` | Examples |
401|---|---|
402| `constitution` | Constitution of Mauritius (`s. 31(2)`) |
403| `statute` | Bail Act 1999 (`s. 4`), Employment Rights Act |
404| `code` | Code Civil Mauricien (`art. 1108`), Code Pénal, Code de Commerce, Code de Procédure Civile |
405| `subsidiary` | Regulations, GN orders, court rules (e.g. Supreme Court (Constitutional Relief) Rules 2000) |
406| `treaty` | International instruments cited as interpretive or persuasive aid — e.g. the European Convention on Human Rights, ICCPR — that do not fit any of the four domestic kinds above |
407
408`jurisdiction` — almost always `"mu"`. Use `"fr"` only when the judgment is directly discussing a French (not Mauritian) statutory text for comparison. For `treaty` entries with no natural single jurisdiction, use `"international"`.
409
410**A cited provision means the text as it stood on the judgment date — never modernize it.** If the judgment cites CCM `art. 1382`, record exactly `art. 1382` even if you believe a reformed French code renumbered the equivalent concept — Mauritius froze its own numbering and did not adopt the French 2016 reform. Never "correct" a provision number toward what you believe a different jurisdiction or era uses.
411
412`foreign_doctrine[]` — French doctrinal secondary sources (Encyclopédie Dalloz, Répertoire Dalloz, Garçon, Garraud, JurisClasseur, named academic authors) are legal authority in this system, not noise, but they are **not** legislation and **not** case citations — capture them in this separate array (`name`, optional `reference`, `line` per §2b), never in `legislation[]` or `cases_cited[]`. The `reference` field is strict-verbatim (§2a): a volume, article, or note number stays exactly as printed even when you suspect an OCR error. **Despite the name, this bucket is not French-only:** named English treatises cited as authority — Archbold, Paley, Taylor on Evidence, Halsbury's Laws of England, and similar named works — belong here on exactly the same basis (e.g. "Halsbury's Laws of England, 4th ed., Vol 1" cited on a point of standing). Detection must not key on French-looking names alone; scan for any named secondary authority, French or English.

414## 9. Procedural posture and dissents
415
416- `procedural_posture` — read the judgment's own self-description ("this is an appeal from...", "ruling on a plea in limine", "interlocutory motion", "constitutional application"). Never infer purely from court/citation format.
417- `judges[]` — bench composition: `name`, `role` (CJ / Senior Puisne Judge / Puisne Judge / Master / Magistrate), `position` (`lead | concurring | dissenting | partial-dissent`), `disposition_agreement` (`full | partial | none`).
418- `separate_judgments[]` — only when a dissent or concurrence carries independent doctrinal weight worth its own record: `judge`, `position`, one-sentence `one_line` summary, `body_range` line span. A bare "I agree" concurrence doesn't need a separate entry — the `judges[]` flag alone covers it.

424## 10. Parties and counsel
425
426`parties{}` — free-form keys by role (plaintiff/defendant/appellant/respondent/co_defendant/intervenor — add roles as the judgment names them). `parties{}` values follow R26 (§3): record each name and its relationship exactly as the source styles them — never synthesize an adversarial pairing the text doesn't state. `counsel[]` — `for` (party represented) and `names[]` (with titles preserved, e.g. "Mr A. Rajah, Senior Attorney").
427
428**Witness-vs-counsel (v2.2):** a witness is never placed in `counsel[]`, even when named in the same passage as counsel. Distinguish by role cue: counsel cues ("appeared for," "of Counsel," "learned counsel for"); witness cues ("called as a witness," "testified," "gave evidence"). When a name's role is ambiguous from the text, omit it from `counsel[]` rather than guess.

430## 11. Structure pointers
431
432`structure.judgment_start_line` / `judgment_end_line` are required; everything else is optional and should be omitted rather than guessed if you can't identify it cleanly. `block_quotes[]` — `start_line`, `end_line`, and `language` (`"fr"`/`"la"` when the quote is French or Latin). `signatures_line` (v2.2 widening) accepts either a single printed line number, or a `[first, last]` two-element range when the signature block genuinely spans multiple lines — use the range form only for a real multi-line block, never to hedge. Remember: all of these numbers come from the printed line-prefixes (§1), never from your own count.
433
434**Document-edge discipline (baseline defect):** `judgment_end_line` is the last line of the judgment text proper — trailing page numbers, bare date lines, and footer artifacts after it are NOT the judgment's end. The baseline absorbed a bare page-number line and a date-only line into `judgment_end_line` on two of five cases (the date line belongs in `date_line`, not in the judgment body range). Verify both edges of every `block_quotes[]` range the same way: a range that starts mid-quote or overshoots into body text corrupts any display layer that dims quotes or suppresses citation extraction inside them.
435
436## 12. Confidence, notes, and the human-review gate
437
438- `confidence{}` — per-field `high | medium | low` for any field involving a genuine judgment call. The fields most likely to need one: `catchwords`, `case_treatments`, `procedural_posture`, `headnote`, `points_of_law`, `court`, `cases_cited`, `legislation`.
439- `notes[]` — free text, one entry per concern. **Whenever any `confidence` entry is `medium` or `low`, write a `notes` entry explaining why** — the flag alone is not self-explanatory to a reviewer.
440
441**R21 — a notes[] entry asserting a specific textual fact must pin it.** Whenever a `notes[]` entry claims something about what the source prints, what was preserved, or what the court did (e.g. "preserved as printed," "the court distinguishes X," "source appears to misprint Y as Z") — embed the line number the claim rests on directly in the note text (e.g. "source L21 prints 'Prosecutions'; retained as printed per R15," not a bare unlocated "preserved as printed"). A textual claim with no pinned line is not flaggable by a reviewer and must not be written. This does not require a note for every field — it requires that *when* you write one making a factual assertion, the assertion is checkable against a specific line, the same way every other claim in this schema already is.
442
443- `needs_human_review` (top-level boolean, always set) — `true` when: a substantive holding can't yield a clean "Whether..." catchword tail; the primary citation is garbled or unresolvable; **the delivery date is not a full ISO date (form 2 `"YYYY"` or form 3 `"0"` under §4's R14 rule — self-enforce the pairing per §4; the validator's CROSSFIELD stage checks it, not the schema)**; a Tier-1 `cases_cited` treatment call is high-stakes and you remain genuinely unsure after applying the rules in §7; or any other Tier-1 field is fundamentally unextractable from the text as given. Otherwise `false`. This is a fast triage gate, separate from and coarser than the per-field `confidence` object.
444
445## 13. Case history chain
446
447`case_history_chain[]` — populate **only** when the judgment text explicitly states the relationship (e.g. "this is an appeal from the decision of the Court of Civil Appeal in [case]"). Do not infer it from citation format or from generic phrases like "the trial court" with no citation given. `relation`: `below | above | concurrent`. `outcome` (optional): `affirmed | reversed | varied | remitted | vacated | set_aside` — populate when the judgment states what happened to the lower-court decision (this is exactly where "reversed" belongs — never in `cases_cited`). `line` follows the same R11 anchor convention as everywhere else (§2b): the printed line number on which the related decision's citation or case-name string, as printed at first mention of the relationship, STARTS. Full appeal-chain assembly beyond what's explicitly stated is a downstream citation-graph concern, not yours.

1683588→ I wish at this stage to quote the case of R v Hungsraz [1970] MR 74, where it
1684589→ was held that :
1685590→ “When considering the question of common purpose, it must not be overlooked
1686591→ that, in principle, its existence does not depend on any agreement between co-
1687592→ delinquents anterior to the commission of any offence. The association of
1688593→ purpose can be formed instantaneously, on the spur of the moment. This is how
1689594→ Garraud aptly describes the situation which is created when several persons
1690595→ unite in the commission of an offence [Trait Théorique et Pratique du Droit Pénal
1691596→ Français, t. 3 p. 4]:
1692597→ ‘Une situation plus pratique est celle des délits commis en réunion, mais sans
1693598→ entente préalable. La participation criminelle suppose une coopération de force
1694599→ et d'activités en vue d'un résultat commun: des individus se réunissent pour
1695600→ commettre un délit, un vol, un assassinat, un empoisonnement.
1696601→ Il y a, la plupart du temps, entre eux, une sorte de convention d'association, ou,
1697602→ s'il est difficile de la dégager, si les coparticipants ont agi sous 1'empire d'une
1698603→ inspiration subite, du moins ils ont en l'intention commune de favoriser, par leur

Headnote

Facts

Garry Michel Simiette was remanded to Beau Bassin Prison on 30 July 2001. While being escorted to Block B, he was assaulted by detainees accused nos 6 and 8, who slapped and kicked him during an altercation over a swindling matter. After being removed to wash his mouth, Simiette was further assaulted by prison officer accused no 1, who kicked and punched him repeatedly on the belly, stomach, and body for approximately 1.5 to 2 minutes. Simiette was taken to Dr Jeetoo Hospital and died on 31 July 2001. Autopsy revealed the cause of death was shock following intra-abdominal bleeding from a ruptured spleen.

Held

  1. Where multiple persons assault a deceased in close temporal and spatial proximity with evidence of shared motivation or common purpose, they are collectively responsible for the death resulting from that assault, even where individual acts vary in severity and the precise fatal blow cannot be attributed to any single perpetrator.
