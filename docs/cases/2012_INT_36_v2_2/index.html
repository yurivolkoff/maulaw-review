587of the same enterprise and linked to each other in time and space.
588I wish at this stage to quote the case of R v Hungsraz [1970] MR 74, where it
589was held that :
590“When considering the question of common purpose, it must not be overlooked
591that, in principle, its existence does not depend on any agreement between co-
592delinquents anterior to the commission of any offence. The association of
593purpose can be formed instantaneously, on the spur of the moment. This is how
594Garraud aptly describes the situation which is created when several persons
595unite in the commission of an offence [Trait Théorique et Pratique du Droit Pénal
596Français, t. 3 p. 4]:
597‘Une situation plus pratique est celle des délits commis en réunion, mais sans
598entente préalable. La participation criminelle suppose une coopération de force
599et d'activités en vue d'un résultat commun: des individus se réunissent pour
600commettre un délit, un vol, un assassinat, un empoisonnement.
601Il y a, la plupart du temps, entre eux, une sorte de convention d'association, ou,
602s'il est difficile de la dégager, si les coparticipants ont agi sous 1'empire d'une
603inspiration subite, du moins ils ont en l'intention commune de favoriser, par leur
604propre activité, celle de leurs compagnons. C'est la participation à un même délit,
605dont la complicité est une modalité.’
606and later, at page 42, when dealing with complicity itself:
607‘Ce qui constitue, un effet, cette dernière situation, c'est l'accord des volontés et
608la fusion des intentions. Cet accord ne se présente pas nécessairement comme
609un fait antérieur au dé1it; ii peut exister dans un moment de passion, subit,
610imprévu; car pour établir entre dé1inquants, 1'entente criminelle qui est
611caractéristique de la complicité, il suffit quelquefois d'un mot, d'un geste, d'un
612regard.’
613In the case of Isaie v The State [2011] SCJ 95, the Supreme Court held that
614where a person is prosecuted under section 228(3) of the Criminal Code, death
615is an incidental element and assault is the vital element to the charge. Therefore
61619
617
618where several persons have assaulted a deceased, they are all collectively
619responsible for the outcome of the assault although the degree of seriousness of
620some blows is greater than others and one cannot say for sure as to who did
621what.
622The essential point is that the aggressors acted in a joint enterprise and satisfy
623the requirements as to “simultaneité d’action” and “assistance reciproque”. Again,
624in the case of Fowdar v R [1950] MR 46, the Supreme Court held,
625“Where persons unite for a common purpose: namely that of inflicting blows upon
626another person and the aggravating circumstance charged was the result of the
627mode of perpetration of the offence by one of the co-authors whilst acting
628simultaneously with and assisting the other, on the basis of the participation of
629the assailants in the aggression and the physical acts done by them during that
630aggression, all the persons charged are guilty of the aggravated offence although
631the actual blow which caused the aggravated circumstance was dealt by only
632one of them.”
633In the case of Paniapen v R [1981] MR 254, Sir Maurice Rault CJ held that if A
634assaults B, and C who passes by and had no previous intention of assaulting B,
635rushes in to join in overpowering B, he becomes a co-author in the assault.
636Applying the above authorities to the facts of the present case, I find that the
637version of Mr Dhunkunsingh was that there had been a fight and the accused
638parties excluding accused no1 had been on the spot at the material point in time.
639Witness Achilles stated that Mr Simiette was hit with the ‘baton mop’, he fell
640down and the prisoners in blue clothes were hitting him. There were about 30 to
64140 prisoners surrounding him like flies on a rotting animal. The version of Mr
642Gooranah was accused no6 slapped him twice and accused no8 who was 3m
643away kicked him ‘en retraite’ because detainee Garry Simiette was of ‘forte
644corpulence’. Then they got afraid of Simiette and dealt him two kicks ‘en retraite’.
645Accused nos 6 and 8 discussed with Simiette as the latter committed a swindling
646against a member of their family.
647From the evidence on record it can be gathered that in his statement to the
648police Mr Gooranah stated that the prisoners assaulted Mr Simiette but in Court
64920
650
651he clarified that he saw accused nos 6 and 8 doing so and he deposed in a
652straightforward and convincing manner and he remained consistent on this

654Furthermore, the evidence revealed that Mr Simiette was removed from the spot
655by Mr Sans Fleur and on the way Mr Simiette was taken to a tap to wash his

657to his address “ tone prend l’argent avec famille dimoune,mo touye toi,liqui to
658mama’. Accused no1 joined in the enterprise which was started by accused nos

660stomach and all over his body and the words uttered by accused no1 showed

663In the case of Alexis v The State [2011] SCJ 81, the Supreme Court held, on
664the issue of co-authorship, that there is no need to prove that the co-authors
665made a prior plan, and that a plan could be reached on the spur of the moment.

672caractéristique de la complicité, il suffit quelquefois d'un mot, d'un geste, d'un
673regard.’
