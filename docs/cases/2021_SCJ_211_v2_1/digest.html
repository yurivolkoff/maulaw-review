12JUDGMENT
13The appellant was prosecuted for involuntary wounds and blows by imprudence in
14breach of Section 239(2) of Criminal Code coupled with Sections 52, 53 and 133(1)(2) of the
15Road Traffic Act (RTA).
16The record reveals that when the case was called before the court for the first time, the
17appellant pleaded guilty following which, the case was fixed for hearing.
18The appellant was inops consilii at the hearing.
19In the course of the hearing the prosecution inter alia produced a statement which the
20appellant had given to the Police, a rough sketch of the locus as well as the PF 58 of witness
21Soobirsen Gobin who had been slightly injured in the accident.
22In his statement to the Police the appellant had admitted liability for the accident and
23upon being informed of the offence committed by him, he accepted the charge and begged for
24excuse.
25
262
27The circumstances of the accident as disclosed in the statement are that on the material
28day at 7.15 a.m. the appellant was driving his car along Royal Road, Belle Vue Maurel from the
29direction of Barlow towards Riviere du Rempart at about 40 kmph on his left side of the road.
30Reaching in the vicinity of Belle Vue Maurel gardens, he slowed down so as to turn right into the
31road leading to Laventure. As he started turning to the right into the said road, his car collided
32with an autocycle coming from the opposite direction. The right front part of his car hit against
33the front part of the autocycle and the autocyclist fell down together with his autocycle. The
34appellant stated that he had not noticed the autocycle prior to the accident.
35The record reveals that at the close of the case the appellant was informed of his rights
36and that he had begged for excuse from the dock.
37At the sentencing stage the appellant admitted a previous conviction which was put to
38him and he was thereafter sentenced to pay a fine of Rs 10,000 and disqualified from holding a
39driving licence for all types of vehicles for a period of 3 months, his driving licence was further
40endorsed and he was ordered to follow a rehabilitation course.
41The appellant has appealed against the disqualification order imposed by the Magistrate;
42the respondent is not resisting the appeal.
43It was submitted on behalf of the appellant that the fine was amply sufficient to meet the
44ends of justice and that there was, in the circumstances, no justification for the disqualification
45order.
46Pursuant to Section 52 coupled with the Second Schedule to the RTA, it is permissible,
47but not mandatory, for the court to impose a disqualification order for a first conviction for an
48offence under Section 133(1) of the RTA with which the appellant was charged in the present
49case.
50However where the court decides, in its discretion, to impose a disqualification order in
51such a case where disqualification is only permissible, the court has to give reasons as to why it
52is exercising its discretion to impose the disqualification order.
53
543
55In the present case the Magistrate proceeded to disqualify the appellant without giving
56any reason as to why she deemed it necessary to do so. The cryptic entry in the record reads
57as follows:
58“I have considered
59- Guilty plea;
60- All evid. on record;
61- Doc X filed;
62- Begs for excuse;
63I sentence the Acc to pay a fine of Rs 10,000 + Acc is disq from
64holding a driving licence for all types of veh for a period of 3
65months & driv lic is endorsed + rehab course + Rs 100 costs.”
66The appellant had no previous conviction for the offence of wounds and blows by
67imprudence with which he stood charged. He had one previous conviction for failing to comply
68with traffic sign (single yellow line) for which he had been fined.
69It was permissible for the court to impose a disqualification order if the circumstances
70inter alia, the degree of the appellant’s imprudence or the seriousness of the accident called for
71such an order.
72In so far as the degree of imprudence is concerned it has been held that:
73“a disqualification should only be ordered against somebody whose
74imprudence was such as to warrant the court to consider that he should,
75in the public interest, be banned from driving for a certain period of time.”
76(Elaheebocus v The State [2013 SCJ 395])
77In the present case the evidence did not disclose any aggravating factor which
78warranted a disqualification order.
79
804
81The appellant’s imprudence consisted in his failure to keep a proper look out for
82oncoming traffic and ascertaining that the road was clear before starting to turn into the side
83road to the right. His imprudence was not per se of a nature as to warrant, in the public interest,
84that he be banned from driving for a certain period.
85The circumstances in the present case may be compared to those in Seetohul v The
86State [2010 SCJ 339]. In Seetohul, the trial court had imposed a six months disqualification
87order with respect to private cars upon the appellant, following his conviction for an offence
88under Section 123(c)(1)(a) of the RTA.
89On appeal the court quashed the disqualification order and made the following
90observation:
91“In the present case the appellant had no previous conviction for the
92offence for which he stood charged. In such a case, disqualification is
93not mandatory but it is permissible. The Magistrate therefore has a
94discretion to impose a disqualification order but such discretion must
95be exercised judiciously after taking into consideration all the
96circumstances of the offence, its degree of seriousness, aggravating
97circumstances if any and the circumstances relevant to the offence.
98If, after weighing all the circumstances of the case, the Magistrate is
99minded to impose a disqualification order, he may do so.”
100In the present case the appellant had no previous conviction for the offence for which he
101was convicted, his imprudence was not of a very serious nature, nor were there any serious or
102aggravating circumstances which warranted the imposition of a disqualification order.
103In the circumstances the Magistrate cannot be said to have made a judicious exercise of
104her discretion to impose a disqualification order. She failed to give any reason as to why she
105considered it necessary to impose a disqualification order and we are left in the dark as to what
106prompted her decision to impose such an order.
107
1085
109For the above given reasons we allow the appeal and quash the disqualification order.
110We further quash the endorsement order inasmuch as pursuant to the Second Schedule to the
111RTA for a first offence under Section 133 an endorsement order is only mandatory where the
112offender is disqualified. The rehabilitation order which follows a disqualification order, cannot
113also stand.
114The endorsement and the rehabilitation orders are accordingly set aside.
115The sentence is otherwise maintained.

Catchwords

  1. Statute (Road Traffic Act) — Disqualification — s. 52 & s. 133(1) — First conviction — Whether reasons must be given when imposing discretionary disqualification
  2. Statute (Road Traffic Act) — Disqualification — Imprudence — Whether imprudence in failing to keep proper lookout warranted disqualification

Headnote

Facts

The appellant, charged with involuntary wounds and blows by imprudence under the Criminal Code and the Road Traffic Act, pleaded guilty. On the material morning at 7.15 a.m., while driving his car along Royal Road at approximately 40 km/h, the appellant turned right into a side road without adequately observing oncoming traffic, causing a collision with an autocycle from the opposite direction. The autocyclist was slightly injured. The appellant admitted liability in his police statement.

Held

  1. For a first conviction under Section 133(1) of the Road Traffic Act, disqualification from holding a driving licence is permissible but not mandatory.

    L46-L49 — Pursuant to Section 52 coupled with the Second Schedule to the RTA, it is permissible, but not mandatory, for the court to impose a disqualification order for a first conviction for an offence under Section 133(1) of the RTA with which the…
    it is permissible, but not mandatory, for the court to impose a disqualification order for a first conviction for an offence under Section 133(1) of the RTA

  2. Where the court exercises its discretion to impose a disqualification order in circumstances where disqualification is only permissible and not mandatory, it must provide reasons for the exercise of that discretion.

    L50-L52 — However where the court decides, in its discretion, to impose a disqualification order in such a case where disqualification is only permissible, the court has to give reasons as to why it is exercising its discretion to impose the disqual…
    where the court decides, in its discretion, to impose a disqualification order in such a case where disqualification is only permissible, the court has to give reasons as to why it is exercising its discretion to impose the disqualification order.

  3. A disqualification should only be ordered where the defendant's imprudence is such as to warrant, in the public interest, that he be banned from driving for a certain period.

    L73-L75 — “a disqualification should only be ordered against somebody whose imprudence was such as to warrant the court to consider that he should, in the public interest, be banned from driving for a certain period of time.”L81-L84 — The appellant’s imprudence consisted in his failure to keep a proper look out for oncoming traffic and ascertaining that the road was clear before starting to turn into the side road to the right. His imprudence was not per se of a nature …
    a disqualification should only be ordered against somebody whose imprudence was such as to warrant the court to consider that he should, in the public interest, be banned from driving for a certain period of time. [...] His imprudence was not per se of a nature as to warrant, in the public interest, that he be banned from driving for a certain period.

Disposition

Appeal allowed. Disqualification order quashed. Endorsement and rehabilitation orders set aside. Sentence otherwise maintained.
