12RULING
13In an Amended Plaint with summons dated November 2015 the plaintiff claims damages of Rs5m
14from the defendant for faute. He avers that he was arrested on 22 and 29 November 2013 on
15provisional charges of aggravated larceny and was remanded, and the police objected to his release
16on bail. The provisional charges were struck out on 27 December 2013 and he was released
17unconditionally. He avers that the decisions of the defendant’s préposé to arrest him and to object to
18bail were capricious, irrational and unreasonable as there was no allegation or allusion or complaint
19made against him in relation to the alleged cases lodged and such acts caused him prejudice.
20On 11 October 2016 attorney for the plaintiff filed a 2nd Proposed Amended Plaint with summons,
21which more specifically seeks to amend paragraphs 11, 13, 18 and 19 to aver that the decisions of
22the defendant to arrest him and to object to bail were “loaded with bad faith” “and there was not an
23iota of evidence justifying his arrest” and that the acts and doings of the defendant constitute faute
24lourde.

27The ground of objection, filed on 6 December 2016, is to the effect that the proposed amendments
28seek to introduce a new cause of action without any notice with regard to the new cause of action
29having been served on the State of Mauritius, in breach of the Public Officers’ Protection Act.
30The court heard arguments on the objection on 10 November 2020.
31Mrs C. Servansing, Senior State Counsel, submitted in a gist that the motion for amendment was
32made more than three years after the alleged acts and the proposed amendments seek to introduce a
33new cause of action in breach of section 4 of the Public Officers Protection Act (“POPA”), that is,
34outside the delay of two years and without any notice being served with respect to the new cause of
35action. The Court has wide powers to allow amendments, but has to be cautious in exercising this
36discretion as it is well settled that the State is liable in tort only where faute lourde is averred - M. A.
37Chung Ching Ah Sue v The State of Mauritius [2015 SCJ 110], Transpacific Export Services Ltd v
38The State and Anor [2018] UKPC 28, K. Dooboree v The State of Mauritius & Anor [2020 SCJ
39207].
40The submissions of Ms R. Umarkhan, for the plaintiff, are in essence that the cause of action has
41remained the same as in the Notice and Plaint with summons (see paragraphs 13 to 15 and 20), so
42that the proposed amendments are not in breach of section 4 of the POPA. There is a distinction
43between the notion of action and the notion of cause of action - M. Mohammad v The State of
44Mauritius & Ors [2014 SCJ 33]. The proposed amendments would not cause any prejudice to the
45defendant, but would bring more precision, clarity to the Amended Plaint with summons – S.
46Mungroo v The Commissioner of Police & Anor [2019 SCJ 72], Transpacific Export Services Ltd
47(supra) - the more so that the case is still at pre-trial stage - Caudan Development Limited v Talbot
48Engineering Co Ltd [SCR 104862 (1/685/10)].
49In reply Mrs Servansing, submitted that the mere omission of the words lourde or bad faith is not
50fatal to a claim, but the facts averred have to constitute faute lourde or bad faith and in the present
51case they do not – H. K. Naga v The Commissioner of Police & Anor [2017 SCJ 286].
52It is not disputed that the court has wide powers to allow amendments “in such manner and on such
53terms as may be just and reasonable” – see Rule 17(1) of the Supreme Court Rules 2000 – and that
54in exercising such powers the court will take into account whether the motion was made at pre-trial
552
56
57stage or during the trial, whether it is substantial and raises new issues inconsistent with the
58pleading, whether there is an explanation for the motion not having been made earlier being given
59that its necessity was apparent well before and whether the amendment will cause prejudice to the
60opposite party which could not be compensated by costs or otherwise – see Harel and Anor v
61Société Jean Claude Harel and Cie and Ors and Société du Patrimoine [1993 MR 251]. I note by
62the way that almost all of the judgments referred to by counsel for the defendant are all on the
63merits when all evidence had already been adduced, whereas the case before me is still at the stage
64of pleadings.
65Still, the overriding principle is that amendments are granted “for the purpose of determining the
66real question in controversy between the parties” – see Rule 17(1) Supreme Court Rules 2000.
67It is also not disputed that the State would only be liable for the acts and doings of its préposés
68where faute lourde is averred and proved, as was said in M. A. Chung Ching Ah Sue and
69Transpacific Export Services Ltd (supra) and I could not agree more with the Privy Council that “It
70is hard to see why… the liability of the police should stand on a footing different from that referable
71to employees of the Revenue Authority”. Be that as it may, in the second cited case the Board also
72said:
73“The mere omission of the words “faute lourde” would not have been fatal to its claim. What,
74however, the company did need to allege were the facts upon which it relied as constituting
75“faute lourde”; and it might at any rate have been helpful if they had been marshalled under
76that specific heading in its plaint. In its plaint, however, the company recited no facts capable
77to constituting “faute lourde”: nor did its evidence establish any.”
78This was cited with approval in K. Dooboree (supra).
79The issues therefore are whether the facts as averred by the plaintiff are capable of constituting
80faute lourde or bad faith and whether the amendments will introduce a new cause of action, this in
81breach of the POPA.
82In both the written notice of the action (Document A – certified copy as produced by Mrs N. D.
83Makhun, Senior Legal Assistant) and the Amended Plaint with summons the plaintiff avers that he
84was arrested and provisionally charged on 22 and 29 November 2013 by police officers acting as
853
86
87préposés of the defendant, the police objected to his release on bail in December 2013, the
88provisional charges against him were struck out on 27 December 2013 upon motion of police
89prosecutor acting as agent of the defendant and that the decisions of the police officers, préposés of
90the defendant, were “capricious, irrational and unreasonable”. He equally avers that during the
91bail hearing the witness representing the defendant said that the police were investigating similar
92cases in which the plaintiff had not been implicated and about which he had not been questioned
93and no incriminating evidence had been found against him.
94I find that the plaintiff has averred facts capable of constituting faute lourde and bad faith and in the
95circumstances the proposed amendments will not introduce any new cause of action, since the
96addition of the words ‘lourde’ and ‘bad faith’ do not detract from the fact that the cause of action
97remains under article 1384 of the Code civil. Therefore, there would be no need for the plaintiff to
98serve another written notice on the defendant, and the issue of breach of the POPA does not arise. I
99find that the proposed amendments will instead bring precision with regard to the determination of
100the real question in controversy.
101I further find that no prejudice will be caused to the defendant inasmuch as the defendant has not
102yet filed its plea.
103For the reasons given above, I set aside the objection raised by the defendant and I allow the
104amendments.
105The case will be mentioned again for the plaintiff to file the Second Amended Plaint with summons.

Headnote

Facts

The plaintiff was arrested on provisional charges of aggravated larceny on 22 and 29 November 2013, remanded in custody, and denied bail by police. The provisional charges were struck out on 27 December 2013 and the plaintiff was released unconditionally. The plaintiff seeks to amend his plaint to allege that the police's decisions were 'loaded with bad faith' and constituted faute lourde, rather than merely 'capricious, irrational and unreasonable'. The State objects that the proposed amendments introduce a new cause of action in breach of section 4 of the Public Officers' Protection Act, which requires notice of a new cause of action.

Held

  1. The addition of 'faute lourde' and 'bad faith' language to a plaint does not introduce a new cause of action where the underlying cause of action remains unchanged under article 1384 of the Code Civil.

Disposition

The objection raised by the defendant is set aside and the amendments are allowed. The case will be mentioned again for the plaintiff to file the Second Amended Plaint with summons.
