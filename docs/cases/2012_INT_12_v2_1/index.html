12JUDGMENT
13The facts
14The plaintiffs’ action was initially against two defendants, but the case against defendant no.2
15was struck out on the 16th December 2009 without any amendment to the body of the proecipe,
16so that defendant no.1 and defendant no.2 are still mentioned therein. La Prudence Mauricienne
17Co Ltd was defendant no.1 and Mr Nawsheally Syed Hossen was defendant no.2 in the pre-
18amended proecipe.
19The claim for damages arises out of a road accident which occurred on the 28th August 2005 at
20Riche Terre, involving vehicle 3528ZP01 insured with plaintiff no.1 and driven/owned by
21plaintiff no.2 and vehicle 2373ZP01 insured with defendant no.1 and owned/driven by defendant
22no.2. The plaintiffs aver that whilst plaintiff no.2 was driving vehicle 3528ZP01 along the New
23Trunk Road at Riche Terre, it was hit from behind by vehicle 2373ZP01 and the impact caused
24vehicle 3528ZP01 to hit the rear of vehicle 1686ZP01. Defendant no.1 is liable as insurer for the
25damage and prejudice suffered by the plaintiffs since the owner/driver of vehicle 2372ZP01 had
26the garde thereof. It is averred in the alternative that the accident occurred through the sole fault
27and/or negligence and/or imprudence of defendant no.2 and defendant no.1 is liable as insurer.
28The defendant admits being the insurer of vehicle 2373ZP01 but denies the other material
29averments in the proecipe, inclusive of the circumstances of the accident as related by the
30plaintiffs. It avers that the accident is a sham and did not occur and that the vehicles involved
31were already damaged in a previous accident and the alleged accident has been made up with a
32view to defraud the insurance company.
33The case for the plaintiffs
34Page | 1
35
36PC Sham said that he examined vehicles 2373ZP01 and “2528ZP01” on the 28th at 15h30 and
37has made a report (Doc. B). There was an accident and the car was damaged, although he cannot
38say how. He only inspected the damaged vehicles.
39The evidence of PC Nagessur is that on the 28th August 2005 he was posted at Terre Rouge
40Police Station and attended to a road accident. The accident involved three vehicles and occurred
41at Riche Terre Road, near the roundabout. He marked the road and the driver came to an
42agreement (Doc. C).
43According to Mr D. K. Molaye, Claim Officer at Phoenix Insurance Ltd, his company was
44informed of the accident by its insured, plaintiff no.2. An estimate of repairs was sent to them
45(Doc. D) and they caused their motor surveyor, Mr Issur, to survey the vehicle (Doc. E). Garage
46Bolivia did the repairs for the sum of Rs187,337 (Doc. F). There was also a survey fee of Rs1380
47and a discharge note signed by Mr “Bano”. He is not pressing for the amount of Rs23,000
48claimed by plaintiff no.2. The accident happened because of the fault of the other drivers and the
49insured was paid the sum stated, which he is now claiming from the defendant. He cannot say if
50the accident never occurred, was a sham and made up with a view to defraud the defendant.
51The case for the defendant
52Mrs A. Sadien, representing the defendant, said that following the claim from Mr Nawsheally
53Syed Hossen on the 31st August 2005, they referred the matter to their expert, Mr Leckraj Ram,
54who gave a report. On the basis of the report to the effect that the circumstances of the accident
55are fraudulous, they did not pay the claim. The accident, if it occurred, was meant to defraud the
56insurance company, so it is not liable towards plaintiff no.1. They gave a declaration at the
57ICAC. She is not aware of the circumstances of the accident and she has not received any
58document from the police or from the ICAC.
59Mr L. Ram, Motor Surveyor, explained that at the request of defendant no.1 he drew a report in
60respect three damaged vehicles (Doc H). His conclusions are that the vehicles did not collide
61with each other. The damages to vehicle 2373ZP01 were to the front only, the bumper had been
62forced in into a ‘V’ as if it hit a pole. Vehicle 1686ZP01 was damaged at the front and rear and
63he was told that the vehicle had been hit at the rear and that the front was damaged in another
64accident. The rear bumper of vehicle 3528ZP01 was damaged. He entertains doubts whether the
65accident occurred as related. He cannot say anything about what the police found. He does not
66know in what order the three vehicles were.
67Discussion
68The plaintiffs’ case is on two limbs, the liability of the defendant as insurer of the custodian of
69vehicle 2373ZP01 under Article 1384 Alinéas 1 and 5 of the Code Civil and its liability as
70insurer for the faute and/or negligence and/or imprudence of the driver of vehicle 2373AP01
71under Articles 1382 and 1383 of the Code Civil.
72Page | 2
73
74Mr G. Bhanji-Soni, counsel for the plaintiffs, submitted that plaintiff no.1 has been subrogated
75into the rights of its insured. This is not contested, but since the plaintiffs are claiming damages
76as a result of a road accident, it is for them to establish that there had been an accident.
77The defendant having admitted that it was the insurer of vehicle 2373ZP01 at the material time,
78and there being no evidence from the defence rebutting the presumption that the owner/driver
79was the gardien at the material time, the defendant is liable unless it succeeds in establishing that
80the accident was due to force majeure or the faute exclusive de la victime. It is to be noted that
81the defendant does not aver any force majeure or faute exclusive de la victime but denies
82paragraph 3 of the proecipe and avers that the accident is a fraud.
83However, I take into consideration the evidence of Mr L. Ram to the effect that he entertains
84serious doubts that the vehicles were involved in the accident as reported by Mr N. S. Hossen
85and plaintiff no.2 and that they collided with each other. It is to be borne in mind that PC sham
86only examined the damaged vehicles and that he did not go to the locus and did not know how
87the alleged accident occurred. He only described what he saw on the vehicles when the accident
88was reported to the police. There is no expert evidence from the plaintiffs to rebut the version of
89Mr L. Ram. I therefore find that his version has remained unrebutted and I accept his evidence
90that there is doubt as to the occurrence of the accident in the light of the damages sustained by
91the vehicles. In fact the whole circumstances of the alleged accident are made suspicious by the
92evidence of the motor surveyor.
93There is a declaration dated 28th August 2005 made by Mr N. S. Hossen, Mrs N. B.
94Fathemamode (plaintiff no.2) and Mr F. Ramjaun in respect of the accident and their not wanting
95police intervention, but I have already ruled that the declaration does not amount to a transaction
96within the meaning of Article 2044 of the Code Civil putting an end to all claims.
97In the light of the above, I find that the defence has been able to cast doubt as to the existence of
98an accident between vehicles 3528ZP01 and 2373ZP01 and as a consequence, I cannot conclude
99that the damages to vehicle 3528ZP01 were not caused by the sole fault of plaintiff no.2.
100Therefore, the plaintiffs’ claim under Article 1384-1 and 1384-5 does not succeed.
101It is clear that the plaintiffs cannot succeed under the second limb of their claim either. Although
102police officers deposed as to the occurrence of the accident and damages to the vehicles, plaintiff
103no.2, the driver of vehicle 3528ZP01 at the material time, was not called to depose in Court. It is
104to be noted that when the case continued on the 8th September 2011 Mr G. Bhanji-Soni, counsel
105for the plaintiffs, informed the Court that he wished to proceed in the absence of plaintiff no.2
106and closed the case for the plaintiffs.
107In Ramphul v La Preservatrice Insurance [1962 MR 56] cited by Mrs S. Carrim, counsel for
108the defendant, the Trial Court had dismissed a claim for damages brought by the insured against
109his insurers. The decision was maintained on appeal. I find pertinent what the Supreme Court
110had to say:
111“We may here remark that in a case of this nature it is for the plaintiff to prove that the
112destruction of the insured property is due to the accidental causes and not for the
113Page | 3
114
115defendant company to adduce evidence to prove that such had been destroyed otherwise
116than by such causes.”
117In the absence of the version of plaintiff no.2 as to the circumstances of the accident, the
118plaintiffs have failed to prove that the accident was as a result of the “sole fault and/or
119negligence and/or imprudence” of the driver of vehicle 2373ZP01. The evidence of the police
120officers is not sufficient to establish the case against the defendant.
121Conclusion
122For all the reasons given above, I find that the plaintiffs have failed to prove the case against the
123defendant on a balance of probabilities.
124I accordingly dismiss the plaintiffs’ action. With costs.
125W. V. Rangan
126Magistrate
127Intermediate Court (Civil Division)
128This 26th January 2012
