4The two applications which have been consolidated are (a) that of a father Jean
5François Lazarre for an order removing the custody of his minor son from the mother and
6entrusting it to him; (b) that of the mother Marie Sianetha Mason for an order granting
7her leave to take the child out of the jurisdiction of this Court. The father is further
8applying for the issue of an interlocutory writ of injunction restraining and prohibiting the
9mother from taking the child out of Mauritius.
10The father and the mother of the child Christophe Lazarre aged 6½ were married
11in South Africa in February 1976. They lived together in that country until 1979 when
12they came to Mauritius with the child. In June 1979, the mother instituted divorce
13proceedings against the father in Mauritius. The latter did not resist the petition. This
14Court pronounced a decree nisi which was subsequently made absolute and it entrusted
15the child to the custody of the mother. It is however clear from the affidavits filed that the
16father never lost interest in his child. After the divorce, the parents expressly or tacitly
17agreed that both of them should share the task and responsibility of maintaining and
18bringing up the child so much so that before the application was made, last October, the
19child was staying with his father during the week from Monday to Friday and dwelt with
20his mother at Tamarin during the end of the week. The arrangements worked
21satisfactorily until September 1982 when the mother remarried Mr. André Lecoultre, an
22electrical engineer. She now intends to leave Mauritius and follow her husband who is
23already in South Africa where he has secured a well remunerated employment. She has
24herself secured employment as a secretary in Johannesburg where she intends to settle.
25She has enrolled the child in a school close to the flat which her husband is already
26occupying in Johannesburg.

28following Articles of the Civil Code:-

30tenant compte exclusivement de leurs avantages et de leurs intérêts.

53I have found the case of Muller v. Masson [1974 MR 268]cited by Counsel very
54useful. I do not agree that it is totally irrelevant to the present case because of the
55enactment of the amendment of the Code Civil by [Act No. 21 of 1981]. For the purpose
56of this case, what that amending Act has done is to give statutory effect to the well known
57and well established principle that in matters of custody the primary consideration should
58be their welfare and interest of the child. The learned Judge in Muller v. Masson made
59the following observations which I find appropriate –
60“In actions relating to the attribution of custody to divorced spouses and to
61the conditions that ought to be imposed upon its exercise, the interests at
62
63stake are those of the child on the one hand, and those of the parents on
64the other. The child’s interests are, by settled rule, the paramount
65consideration. They need not, however, be in conflict with and are not
66normally closely linked with those of either or both of the parents since it
67is commonplace and commonsense that the welfare of the child requires
68that he, or she, should keep as many ties as reasonable possible with both
69father and mother. This is particularly so in an application like the one
70before me when the parent vested with the custody contemplates
71emigrating with the child. For that parent, the reason for taking up
72residence in a foreign land would almost invariably be that he or she
73expects to find there conditions of life more favourable than those
74obtaining at home. For the other parent, the ground of opposition would
75usually be the vindication of his or her rights of access to, and of
76“surveillance” over, the child. The latter rights are conferred by law and
77should, therefore, be protected to the utmost.
78(See Sababady v. Rivet [1972 MR 171]and the authorities cited there). But
79the parent to whom custody has been granted has none the less the right to
80conduct his or her life and insure his or her future as he or she deems
81appropriate, and that right, if reasonably exercised, should not be lightly
82restricted. The problem is to reconcile all those interests to the best
83advantage of the child.”
84After citing two decisions one English and the other French on the subject matter, he
85made the following observation:
86The passage quoted are well illustrative of the meticulous care and the
87anxiety with which judges would naturally investigate a request by a
88parent who has been given custody which, if granted, would have for
89result to curtail in some significant measure the right of the other parent to
90have access to his child. This is even more so when the parent who has
91been given custody has remarried or intends to remarry, since the nature of
92the relations between the child and the stepfather or stepmother, or the
93future husband or wife, is an all important factor. In France some courts
94
95have even thought that remarriage was a ground for depriving a spouse of
96custody, but the general tendency, which is more consonant with logic, is
97to enquire whether, in view of the change in circumstances arising from
98the remarriage, the maintaining of custody to the remarried spouse would
99not be prejudicial to the interests of the child [see the note to the judgment
100of the Cour d’Appel de Paris (supra) ]. (The underlining is mine)
101Vide also Karvonen v. Celine (1978) Judgment No. 227 which makes useful
102reading although the facts were dissimilar. The fact that one of the parents has been
103entrusted with the custody of the child by a Court is certainly one of the elements which
104the Court which has to decide the issue must take into account but the Court must guard
105itself against the danger of giving undue importance to that aspect so as not to
106overshadow the new circumstances which have arisen and which may call for a
107revocation and modification of an order already made. In the present case the father has
108affirmed that he did not resist the petition because there was no hope for the parties to
109reconcile and he was unwilling to have resort to mudslinging in the witness box but it is
110useful to note that the order of custody which was made by the Court in favour of the
111mother was radically modified and, thus modified, implemented and respected by both
112divorced spouses.
113Now what are the important and useful elements which are brought out by the
114evidence in this case?
115The father with whom the child has been and still is living has no doubt a good
116situation by local standard. He is quite able to support and maintain the child, give him a
117good education, bestow all paternal love and affection on him and, with the help of his
118own parents, allow him to grow up in an ambiance to which he is already used. He has
119undertaken to send the child to the maternal grand parent at Rose Belle during the
120weekends and to let the mother have him all to herself when she is in Mauritius. Having
121heard and seen him, I have no reason to doubt that he will keep his promise.
122I have no doubt that the mother is also devoted to her son and sincerely wants his
123welfare and progress. She has spoken of the bright future which the child will have if he
124is taken to South Africa now. She and her husband certainly have the means to look after
125the child. Her husband has sworn a brief affidavit in which he has affirmed that he has
126
127known the child to whom he is attached for about 2 years and that he is quite willing to
128provide for his maintenance, education and future but, for reasons which have not been
129elaborated or explained he left Mauritius for South Africa before the hearing. I did not
130have the opportunity to hear him or know more about him and properly assess to what
131extent he would be ready and willing to welcome the child in his family. At the outset of
132the hearing, learned counsel of the father drew my attention to the impossibility of his
133cross examining that witness which he must have thought was essential since the relation
134between the child and his stepfather is a very important element to be considered if the
135child remains with his mother.
136The letter dated 1st October 1982 which the mother wrote to the father after the
137present proceedings had started in which she made a passionate appeal to him in an
138eleventh hour effort to persuade him to release the boy and in which she appears to have
139disclosed the inmost recess of her heart is also very illuminating. The following passages
140of that letter reveal to what extent she has committed herself to the idea of leaving her
141homeland and follow her husband regardless of what she called her personal problems.
142“ Attacquons maintenant la situation bien en face. Au point ou j’en
143suis je ne peux plus reculer. J’ai refait ma vie et l’homme que j’ai choisi
144pour refaire ma vie ne peux payer mes erreurs passées. Je ne peux venir
145subitement entravaer son avenir par mes problemes personels. Il n’y est
146pour rien. ……………
147Moi comme je t’ai expliqué il est trop tard pour que je
148recule – je me suis mariée à quelqu’un qui n’est pas responsable de tout ce
149qui m’arrive. Il m’a laissé choisir depuis longtemps et je lui dis d’aller de
150l’avant. Il se trouve que maintenant il a perdu son job ici et il s’est engagé
151en Afrique du Sud où un nouveau job l’attend. Je n’ai pas le droit de briser
152son avenir. Il doit partir. Moi je n’ai plus rien ici sinon ma valise. Mes
153parents comme tu le sais s’en vont l’année prochaine. Les temps se font
154durs. Tout est cher. Les firmes ferment leurs portes. La vie ici devient de
155plus en plus difficile. C’est pas la peine de t’en dire d’avantage.”
156
157I have come to the conclusion that on the evidence adduced before me allowing
158the child to leave Mauritius for South Africa is too much like a leap in the dark and is too
159hazardous and risky an undertaking in so far as the interest of the child is concerned.
160I therefore reject the motion of the mother. I order that the child Christophe
161Lazarre be removed from the custody of the mother and entrusted to his father Jean
162Francois Lazarre. I further order the issue of a writ of injunction restraining and
163prohibiting the mother from taking the child out of the jurisdiction of this Court.

Headnote

Facts

The parents Jean François Lazarre and Marie Sianetha Mason married in South Africa in 1976 and had a son Christophe, aged 6½. They relocated to Mauritius in 1979, and the mother instituted divorce proceedings in June 1979, to which the father did not oppose; the court awarded custody to the mother. After the divorce, the parents established an arrangement whereby Christophe lived with his father Monday to Friday and with his mother at Tamarin on weekends, which arrangement worked satisfactorily. In September 1982, the mother remarried André Lecoultre, an electrical engineer, and intends to relocate to South Africa to follow her husband, having secured employment as a secretary in Johannesburg and enrolled the child in a local school. The father applies for modification of the custody order and an interlocutory injunction to prevent removal; the mother seeks leave to take the child out of Mauritius.

Held

  1. Article 261 of the Code Civil establishes that the primary and paramount consideration in custody determinations must be the welfare and interests of the child.
