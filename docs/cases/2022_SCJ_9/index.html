13RULING
14The plaintiff has entered a plaint with summons (PWS) on 10 November 2015 claiming
15damages against both defendants in the amount of Rs.135,100,000/- for their wrongful acts and
16doings. Defendants are resisting the PWS and have also raised a plea in limine litis which
17reads as follows –
18“Defendants move that the Plaint with Summons be dismissed with costs
19inasmuch as the action is time barred under section 4(1) of the Public
20Officers Protection Act.”
21Ex facie the plaint, the events unfold as follows:-
22(i) On 21 August 2001, the plaintiff applied for an Environment Impact Assessment
23(EIA) licence for the collection and re-refining of used lube oil from defendant
24no.2 at the location site of Mare Anguilles, Mont Blanc;
25(ii) On 26 June 2002, the plaintiff was informed that a Technical Advisory Committee
26had been set up to look into its application;
27
282
29(iii) On 7 July 2003, the EIA licence was not approved and plaintiff appealed against
30that decision;
31(iv) On 31 July 2006, the then Environment Appeal Tribunal (EAT) remitted the EIA
32licence application back to defendant no.2 for reconsideration;
33(v) On 14 March 2008, the Technical Advisory Committee renewed its decision of 20
34June 2003 in not recommending the project;
35(vi) On 16 July 2010, the EIA licence application was not approved and the plaintiff
36was asked to submit a fresh application;
37(vii) On 06 January 2011, the plaintiff requested the then Ministry of Housing and
38Lands to reconsider an application for a plot of land for the recycling of used oils;
39(viii) On 14 November 2011, plaintiff was informed that his request for a plot of land
40was being considered by the said Ministry;
41(ix) On 17 November 2011, the then Ministry of Housing and Lands enquired as to
42the surface area of plot of land required;
43(x) On 6 June 2012, the plaintiff was asked by the then Ministry of Housing and
44Lands for more particulars about its project;
45(xi) On 12 June 2012, the plaintiff informed the then Ministry of Housing and Lands
46that the particulars sought were being attended to by its consultants;
47(xii) On 11 September 2014, the plaintiff submitted a new and fresh EIA licence
48application with the site located at Cluny;
49(xiii) From 28 January 2015 until the period of 04 March 2015, there was a request for
50and submission of information by the plaintiff to the defendant no.2; and
51(xiv) On 23 July 2015, an EIA licence was issued;
52At paragraphs 29, 30 and 31 of the PWS, the following were averred –
53“29. The Plaintiff verily believes that since August 2001, it has been denied
54an EIA licence without any reasonable cause or lawful justification and
55was motivated by lack of good faith.
5630. The Plaintiff has sustained the following tortious, illegal, negligent and
57unlawful acts and doings of the Defendant No.2 –
58(a) arbitrary refusal of the 1st EIA application of 2001;
59
603
61(b) the Plaintiff has been denied of the right of an
62administrative justice by the delay and bureaucratic red
63tapism;
64(c) lack of “bona fide” in due processing of the first
65application, resulting in an unjustified rejection of the
66application of EIA licence;
67(d) the delay in processing the matter following judgment
68of Environment Appeal Tribunal;
69(e) the “manque à gagner” resulting from the delay loss
70suffered to equipment purchased which became
71worthless scraps.
7231. The Plaintiff avers that by unlawfully and illegally denying it an EIA
73licence ever since August 2001, the Defendant No.2 has committed a
74“faute” and as a result of the said tortious acts and doings, the Plaintiff
75has suffered great hardship, considerable prejudice, loss & “manque à
76gagner” and damage, which it values at Rs.135,100,000/-…..”
77According to learned counsel for the defendants, the cause of action arose on 10 July
782010 when the plaintiff was informed that the EIA licence application had not been approved.
79Since the PWS was lodged in 2015, it is time barred under section 4(1) of the Public Officers
80Protection Act (POPA) as it should have been lodged within the 2 years of the cause of action
81arising.
82Learned counsel for the plaintiff submitted that the cause of action arose on 23 July
832015 regarding the administrative decision of the defendant no.2. However, counsel for the
84plaintiff added that the claim for damages is a result of the acts and doings of the defendant
85no.2 since 2001. She qualified the mention of the words “since August 2001” being not in
86relation to when the cause of action arose but rather to show and indicate the delay in the
87defendant no.2 taking a decision only in 2015. She also submitted that ex facie the plaint, the
88plea in limine cannot be adjudicated upon as it is impossible to ascertain when or at what stage
89defendant no.2 committed a tortious act. Evidence will need to be ushered in so as to prove the
90delay taken in granting an EIA licence in 2015 and which amounts to a “faute”.
91Both learned counsel referred to various authorities regarding the operation of section
924(1) of POPA. I have considered the submissions of both learned counsel. The plea in limine
93either fails or wins the day depending on when the cause of action arose as per the PWS
94entered by the plaintiff. The plaintiff has the carriage of proceedings and it is a fundamental
95
964
97legal principle that the plaintiff is bound by its pleadings. It decided to lodge a PWS in 2015 for
98events arising since 2001.
99On a careful reading of paragraphs 29 to 31 of the PWS and more specifically at
100paragraph 30 (a), it is the “arbitrary refusal of the 1st EIA application” which is being contested,
101the plaintiff even qualified the decision to smack of bad faith as per paragraph 30 (b). This
102would be in relation to the first application for an EIA licence which was first rejected in 2003
103and again in 2010. At paragraph 30 (c), the plaintiff referred to the delay taken following the
104judgment of the EAT which again refers to the first application for an EIA licence. The directive
105of the EAT was that the Ministry was called upon to reconsider the first EIA application that the
106plaintiff made for a licence. Finally, when the application was reconsidered, the plaintiff still did
107not obtain an EIA licence in 2010. It was instead asked to submit a new and fresh application in
1082010.
109Learned counsel for the plaintiff had unconvincingly submitted that it could not be
110ascertained ex facie the plaint when the cause of action arose. This is contradictory to the
111averments of the plaint which clearly set out that the plaintiff’s case is that it had been denied an
112EIA licence since 2010 following an application made in 2001.
113In relation to the EIA licence granted in 2015, it was in relation to an application made in
1142014 with a different location site, that is, Cluny and not as per the location site in the first EIA
115licence application in 2001. Administrative delay which the plaintiff had averred in its plaint
116could not be in relation to the EIA licence obtained in 2015 following a fresh application made in
1172014. Ex facie the plaint, the alleged administrative delay which the plaintiff had referred to in
118its plaint can only refer to what took place in 2010 when the EIA application was made since
1192001 had been rejected for a second time.
120I therefore agree with learned counsel for the defendants that the cause of action arose
121in 2010 following the defendant no.2 rejecting the application for an EIA licence which the EAT
122had referred to since 2006. In entering the PWS in 2015 after it had obtained an EIA licence in
1232015 following a fresh application made in 2014 shows the erroneous stand of the plaintiff in
124believing that –
125
1265
127(i) it was within the statutory limitation period of entering a case against the State,
128and
129(ii) in accordance with POPA for a cause of action arising in 2010.
130Further, paragraphs 30 (a) to (e) of the PWS clearly refer to the first application for an
131EIA licence. It has also been set out at paragraph 31 of the PWS that the EIA licence had been
132denied since August 2001. Therefore, ex facie the plaint, it can also be construed that the case
133of the plaintiff is that it should have obtained the EIA licence since 2001. This is even worse
134and more damaging to the plaintiff’s case as regards the limitation period for suing the State as
135the PWS was entered in 2015.
136As regards the authorities referred by learned counsel for the plaintiff to support its case
137that the plea in limine is premature and evidence needs to be ushered in, the present case is
138distinguishable from Consolidated Steel Ltd v The State of Mauritius [2010 SCJ 344]. Ex
139facie the plaint, the plaintiff clearly averred that it had been denied an EIA licence since 2001
140and in relation to that application, the final decision was given by defendant no.2 in 2010 and
141not in 2015. It is salient to note that in view of the change in the location site where the intended
142project was to be set up, the application of 2001 clearly differs to the application made in 2014
143and which explains that the respondent was requested to make a fresh application thereof. As
144regards the case of Cono Cono & Co Ltd v Black River District Council [2015 SCJ 212], this
145was a plaint against the district council which is governed by a different regime set out in section
14643(1) of the Local Government Act for legal suits entered against it. I find that it is not relevant
147to this plea in limine.
148As per the PWS, the cause of action arose in 2010 and not in 2015. Considering that ex
149facie the PWS, the plea in limine can be determined on the basis of section 4(1) of the POPA
150and the authorities of the Supreme Court which held that it is mandatory to comply with the
151limitation period of 2 years to sue the State, I uphold the plea in limine of the defendants and
152accordingly set aside the PWS with costs.
